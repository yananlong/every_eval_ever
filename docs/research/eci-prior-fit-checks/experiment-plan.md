## Orientation for a New Reader

This section explains the objects the plan refers to. Every statement about Epoch's code was checked against `ext/eci-public` at commit e04929a, and every count against the data release in `ext/epoch_zip/benchmark_data.zip`.

**Epoch AI and the ECI.** Epoch AI is a research organisation that collects benchmark scores for AI language models. Its Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) summarises each model's scores on many benchmarks as one number. A model that scores higher on harder benchmarks gets a higher ECI. The companion number for a benchmark is its difficulty index (EDI).

**The data the model is fitted to.** The fit input has one row per (model, benchmark) pair with a `performance` value in $[0, 1]$. A raw score is normalised as $(\mathrm{raw} \times \mathrm{scale} - \mathrm{random\_baseline}) / (\mathrm{score\_ceiling} - \mathrm{random\_baseline})$ and clipped to $[0, 1]$, so 0 means chance level and 1 means the benchmark's stated ceiling; the baseline and ceiling per benchmark are in `benchmark_metadata.csv`. A "model" is a model group: `model_metadata.csv` maps 1,227 model versions to groups, and the group's score on a benchmark is the best score of any of its versions. Epoch keeps rows dated 2023-01-01 or later, drops a benchmark's older variant where a group also has the newer one, and keeps a model only if it has at least 4 benchmarks and at least 4 benchmarks with a score in $[0.1, 0.9]$ (called mid-range in this plan). The published fit input, `epoch_capabilities_index/processed_data_for_eci.csv`, has 2,879 rows for 256 models and 57 benchmarks (58 benchmarks are flagged `in_eci`; SuperGLUE has no qualifying rows). Rows per model range from 4 to 29 (median 10); 72 models have 6 or fewer. 59 scores are exactly 0 and 22 are exactly 1.

**The model.** For model $m$ with capability $c_m$ and benchmark $b$ with difficulty $d_b$, slope $a_b > 0$ and noise level $\sigma_b$:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{mb} &= \operatorname{sigmoid}\bigl(a_b \times (c_m - d_b)\bigr), \quad \text{clipped to } [1\mathrm{e}{-6},\ 1 - 1\mathrm{e}{-6}];\\
\kappa_b &= \frac{1}{4\sigma_b^2} - 1;\\
y_{mb} &\sim \operatorname{Beta}\bigl(\mu_{mb} \times \kappa_b,\ (1 - \mu_{mb}) \times \kappa_b\bigr),
\end{aligned}
```

where $y$ is the performance clipped to $[0.001, 0.999]$ (`performance_clip_eps = 1e-3`).

The Beta's standard deviation is $2\sqrt{\mu (1 - \mu)}\,\sigma_b$, so $\sigma_b$ is the noise SD where the curve passes 0.5 and the noise shrinks toward 0 and 1. This is a one-dimensional item response model with a Beta likelihood.

**The priors** (class `BayesianPriors` and function `build_bayesian_model` in `src/eci/bayesian.py`; "$\operatorname{LogNormal}(m, s)$" has log-mean $m$ and log-SD $s$):

| Parameter | Prior | Comment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $c_m$ (capability) | $\operatorname{Uniform}(-50, 50)$ | flat; not configurable |
| $d_b$ (difficulty) | flat on the subspace where the $d_b$ sum to 0, each bounded to $[-50, 50]$ | the sum fixes the location of the scale |
| $\log a_b$ (log slope) | ZeroSumNormal with SD $\tau_\alpha \times \sqrt{K / (K - 1)}$, $K = 57$ | log slopes sum to 0, so the geometric-mean slope is 1; this fixes the unit of the scale |
| $\tau_\alpha$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln 0.5, 0.5)$ | spread of log slopes |
| $\sigma_b$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln \sigma_{\mathrm{median}}, \tau_\sigma)$ truncated to $(0, 0.5)$ | hierarchical noise SD per benchmark |
| $\sigma_{\mathrm{median}}$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln 0.1, 0.5)$ | typical noise SD |
| $\tau_\sigma$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln 0.5, 0.5)$ | spread of log $\sigma_b$ |

Only the three hyperpriors ($\tau_\alpha$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{median}}$, $\tau_\sigma$) can be changed through Epoch's interface. A joint draw from these priors is not a plausible dataset: about 89% of prior-simulated scores fall at the clip bounds and about 5% in $[0.1, 0.9]$ (a calculation from the review, repeated and extended in stage W1). The flat priors are meant to be uninformative, not generative.

**The sampler.** `fit_bayesian_eci` runs NUTS (the standard adaptive Hamiltonian Monte Carlo sampler in PyMC) with 4 chains, target acceptance 0.95 and default seed 12345. It first runs a pilot with a diagonal mass matrix (1,000 tuning steps and 1,000 draws per chain), estimates the parameter covariance from it, and then samples with that dense mass matrix (500 tuning steps; 1,000 draws per chain, increased up to 16,000 until the checks pass). If that stage has any divergence or still fails at 16,000 draws, it falls back to a "cold" diagonal run (4,000 up to 32,000 draws). The convergence checks are: zero divergent transitions, $\mathrm{r\_hat} \le 1.01$ and bulk effective sample size (ESS) $\ge 400$ for capability, difficulty, slope, sigma and the three hyperparameters. Failures produce warnings, not errors. Epoch's README quotes about two minutes per fit.

**The anchors and the ECI scale.** The raw scale (c, d) has no meaningful unit, so every posterior draw $s$ is mapped linearly so that Claude 3.5 Sonnet sits at 130 and GPT-5 at 150:

```math
\mathrm{ECI}_m^{(s)} = 130 + 20 \times \frac{c_m^{(s)} - c_{\mathrm{low}}^{(s)}}{c_{\mathrm{high}}^{(s)} - c_{\mathrm{low}}^{(s)}},
```

with $\mathrm{low}$ = Claude 3.5 Sonnet and $\mathrm{high}$ = GPT-5. Difficulties are mapped the same way to give EDI, and slopes are divided by the per-draw scale factor. The published ECI is the mean of $\mathrm{ECI}_m^{(s)}$ over draws, and the published 90% credible interval is the 5th and 95th percentile. The two anchors have no interval because their values are fixed by definition. A consequence: any change that moves either anchor's raw capability relative to the other models moves every published score.

**What Epoch publishes** (folder `epoch_capabilities_index/` in the zip): `eci_scores.csv` (ECI and interval per model), `edi_scores.csv` (EDI and scaled slope per benchmark, without intervals or sigma), `eci_bootstraps.json` (500 posterior draws of every model's ECI and every benchmark's EDI and slope) and the fit input. The published 90% interval widths range from 2.9 to 41.8 ECI points (median 5.3); for the 20 highest-ranked models they are 4.2 to 7.6.

**Epoch's diagnostic suite** (`src/eci/evaluation.py`, run with `evaluate=True`): sampler diagnostics; accuracy of the fitted curve; PSIS-LOO (an importance-sampling approximation to leave-one-out cross-validation) with Pareto-k reliability bands; posterior predictive p-values, per benchmark and pooled, for nine statistics of the score distribution (mean, sd, variance, IQR, min, max, skew, share below 0.05, share above 0.95); and PIT calibration with coverage at 50, 80, 90 and 95%, in-sample and under PSIS-LOO. It has no prior predictive check, no prior sensitivity analysis, and no check of dimensionality, curve shape, time drift or developer effects.

**The workflow framing.** The first version of this plan audited one fixed model in four separate blocks. A Bayesian workflow (Gelman, Vehtari, McElreath et al. 2026, *Bayesian Workflow*, Chapman & Hall/CRC) treats the same activities as stages of one iteration on one model: simulate data from the priors and look at what they imply (Ch. 5, prior predictive checking); fit and verify the computation; check the sampler and the model by simulating data from known parameters and refitting (simulation-based calibration, Ch. 14 and 31); compare the fitted model's predictions with the data (Ch. 8, posterior predictive and LOO-PIT checks); see how far the conclusions depend on the priors (Ch. 8, Section 8.5, with a worked example in Ch. 17); and, only where the checks show a gap, expand the model and compare the versions by predictive accuracy (Ch. 24 and 25; Ch. 4 develops the same steps on an item response model for a multiple-choice exam, the family Epoch's model belongs to). Block W follows that order as six stages, W1 to W6 (the stage table in Experimental Storyline lists them). The stages share one fit of Epoch's model (W2) and one set of 199 refits of simulated data (W3), because each stage asks a different question of the same objects: the 199 refits give both a calibration check of the sampler and model and the reference distribution for the structural checks, and the W2 fit gives the posterior for the predictive checks, the power-scaling screen and the baseline against which expansions are compared. Sharing also removes repeated fitting, which is the main cost (every fit uses all 4 CPUs). The registered verdicts C1 (prior robustness), C2 (model adequacy) and C3 (transfer to independent scores) keep their thresholds and rules and are decided on Epoch's model as published. Everything the workflow adds beyond them, namely the prior predictive study and the reference prior (W1), the targeted prior refits (W5) and the model expansions (W6), is labelled exploratory and cannot change a verdict.

**EEE.** Every Eval Ever (EEE) is an open dataset that stores published evaluation results from many sources in one JSON schema. The manifest used here (`ext/eee/entries.jsonl`, core hash dd7efcff, created 2026-11-13) lists 122,861 aggregate records in 129 collections. One record is one model evaluated by one source; it carries `source_metadata` (who produced the number), `model_info` (model id and name) and a list `evaluation_results`, each with a name, a `metric_config` (unit, minimum, maximum) and `score_details.score`.

**Same-upstream versus independent.** An EEE score and an Epoch score for the same model and benchmark are same-upstream when both were copied from one original measurement, for example the ARC Prize leaderboard, which Epoch cites and EEE also ingests. Agreement between them only shows that the crosswalk matched the right rows. They are independent when the EEE source ran the benchmark itself, separately from the measurement Epoch used. Only independent pairs test whether ECI predicts scores obtained elsewhere. The exact classification rule is in M9.

**Terms used below.**

- Posterior predictive check: simulate data from the fitted model and compare a statistic of the simulated data with the same statistic of the real data.
- PIT (probability integral transform): the share of the model's predictive draws at or below the observed value. A calibrated model gives uniform PIT values; a central 90% interval covers the observation exactly when $0.05 \le \mathrm{PIT} \le 0.95$.
- Coverage: the share of observations covered by their interval.
- Prior predictive check: simulate parameters from the priors, then data from those parameters, and ask whether the simulated scores look like scores a benchmark could produce.
- Simulation-based calibration (SBC): draw parameters, simulate data, refit, and compute the rank of each true parameter among its posterior draws; if the sampler and model are correct the ranks are uniform. Here the parameters are drawn from the posterior fitted to the real data rather than from the prior, so the check is conditional on the observed data (M13 states what that changes).
- ELPD, PSIS-LOO and K-fold: the expected log predictive density is the model's average log probability of new scores; PSIS-LOO estimates it by importance sampling without refitting, and its Pareto $\hat{k}$ diagnostic marks rows where the estimate is unreliable ($\hat{k} > 0.7$); K-fold refits on training folds and scores the held-out rows directly.
- Power-scaling sensitivity (psens): a diagnostic that reweights the existing posterior draws as if the prior had been raised to a power slightly different from 1 and measures how far a quantity's distribution moves (Kallioinen et al. 2024).
- Family-wise error rate: the probability of at least one false rejection among a set of tests.
- Simulation apparatus: the code that simulates data from a known model, refits it and scores the checks (stages W3 and W4).
- Stage names: W2a, W2b, W4a to W4d were B0a, B0b, B3a to B3d in the first version; B1 became W3, B2 became W5, and B4 to B6 keep their names. The Revision Log rows R1 to R16 use the old names.

## Context

- Problem: Epoch's public code switched the ECI to a Bayesian model (merge commit e04929a, 2026-11-03) and Epoch now reports 90% credible intervals. Neither the ECI page nor the repository reports a prior sensitivity analysis or any posterior predictive check results. The repository ships a diagnostic suite behind `--evaluate` (described above) that has no prior predictive check, no prior sensitivity, and no checks aimed at the assumptions a one-dimensional index depends on.
- Evaluation goal: decide whether the published scores and intervals survive defensible prior changes (C1), whether the model fits the data in the ways that matter for the index (C2), and whether its intervals predict independently sourced scores held in EEE (C3). A fourth, exploratory question, tied to no registered claim, is whether any pre-declared model expansion predicts the data clearly better and, if so, how much the scores change under it (W6).
- Operating mode: standalone.
- Upstream artifacts used: our Composite Index Model v1 spec (revision 3), which already describes ECI's model, and the v1 fit results (its cross-source coverage was 0.70; its local-dependence test failed). The v9 fit scripts and outputs were lost when the container was reset, so only numbers recorded in the conversation carry forward.
- Main constraints: 4 CPUs and 15 GB RAM. The fitting set is small (57 benchmarks, 256 models, 2,879 observations). Each fit uses all 4 CPUs (one chain per core), so fits run one after another; at Epoch's quoted two minutes per fit the plan needs about 13.5 hours of sampling without W6, of which about 10 are the restartable simulations in W3. - Critical reviewer concern: a clean result may come from perturbations that are too small, metrics that average over well-measured models, or checks that cannot reject. W3 exists to rule this out.
- Current evidence class: exploratory.
- Requested evidence class: confirmatory for C1 and C2 (pre-registered thresholds, self-reviewed, no independence claimed); exploratory for C3. W1, W6, the reference-prior entry PR and the targeted refits of W5, and every result under an adopted expansion, are exploratory.

## Non-Vacuity Preflight

- Discriminating case: in the published results the models ranked 2 to 9 lie within 3.3 ECI points of each other, and the published draws give adjacent-pair ordering probabilities between 0.52 and 0.78 for seven of the nine adjacent pairs in the top 10. A prior that moves one of these models by 1.5 to 1.9 points (a quarter of its interval width) trips S2 and can reorder them, which S4 scores. Claude Fable 5.2 (19 scores, 8 mid-range) leads Gemini 4 Carbon (14 scores, 7 mid-range) by 3.68 points with 90% intervals 7.6 and 7.0 points wide; reversing that pair needs a relative shift of 3.7 points. The frontier stratum is scored on its own, so shifts there cannot be averaged away.
- Plausible comparator win: W3's positive-control prior (slopes forced nearly equal) must fail C1's rule; W3's planted misfits must be flagged by their targeted checks; in W1 the reference prior PR must pass its pre-declared plausibility criterion while Epoch's priors are reported against the same criterion; in W6 an expansion can win only by the stated elpd rule, and the base model is reported as adequate if none does; in B4, same-upstream pairs must match and EEE-vs-EEE agreement sets the achievable bar.
- Complete loss or outcome contract: non-converged fits, unresolved grid entries, stress priors, non-converged simulation replicates, every crosswalk class, clipped scores and every W6 candidate (preferred or not) are all reported with counts.
- Case-selection independence: the prior grid (`prior-grid.json`), check list, statistics, strata, candidate expansions and thresholds were fixed before any refit. The frontier and sparse strata are defined from the published files, not from our fits.
- Skip, failure, null, and retry accounting: Epoch's own draw ladder runs inside every fit; a W5 fit that still fails gets one retry with draws doubled and is then recorded as unresolved, not dropped; a W6 candidate follows its own pre-declared fallback (M14) and is reported as "not fitted" if that fails.
- Attainability check (added in review): every numeric gate was checked to be reachable and not automatic. Three were not and were changed (Revision Log R3, R6, R8). The restructure added numeric rules in W1, W3 (SBC), W5 and W6; the reference-prior criterion was checked by simulation on the observation pattern (PR: mean mid-range share 0.45, 98.5% of datasets at or above 0.15, mean clip-bound share 0.26, against the criterion 0.40, 95% and 0.35; Epoch's priors: 0.05, 0.2% and 0.89), while the SBC and W6 thresholds cannot be checked before fits and are listed in Open Judgement Calls 11 to 15.
- Gate result: pass.

## Methods Specification

The parts are numbered M1 to M14 and are cited from the JSON files. Unless stated, "draws" means all retained posterior draws pooled over chains, and "the fit input" means `processed_data_for_eci.csv`.

### M1. Quantities on the anchored scale

- ECI draw: $\mathrm{ECI}_m^{(s)}$ as defined in the orientation, computed per draw from the raw `capability` draws. Epoch's function returns these as `draws["eci"]` (shape draws x models).
- Point estimate $\mathrm{ECI}_m$: mean over draws. Interval: 5th and 95th percentile over draws (numpy `quantile`, default interpolation). Width $W_m$: 95th minus 5th percentile. $W_{m,0}$ is the width in the baseline fit.
- Shift of model $m$ under prior $k$: $\Delta_{m,k} = \mathrm{ECI}_{m,k} - \mathrm{ECI}_{m,0}$. Relative shift: $\lvert\Delta_{m,k}\rvert / W_{m,0}$.
- Width ratio: $W_{m,k} / W_{m,0}$.
- $P(i > j)$: the share of draws $s$ with $\mathrm{ECI}_i^{(s)} > \mathrm{ECI}_j^{(s)}$, using the same draw index for both models.
- Anchors: Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-5 have ECI fixed at 130 and 150 in every draw. They are included in rank-based metrics (S1, S4) and excluded from shift, width and psens metrics. There are 254 non-anchor models.
- Raw-scale versions of the shift and width use `capability` draws directly; they are reported as secondary and do not enter any rule.

### M2. Strata

Fixed from the published files before any fit and stored in `runs/strata.json`:

- all: the 256 models (254 non-anchor).
- frontier: the 20 models with the highest `eci` in the published `eci_scores.csv` (Claude Fable 5.2 down to GPT-5.5; published ECI 173.44 to 160.10). None is an anchor and none is sparse.
- sparse: the 72 models with 6 or fewer rows in the fit input.
- dense: the 184 models with 7 or more rows (182 non-anchor). Reported; used only to state the narrowed form of C1.

### M3. Sensitivity metrics S1 to S5

Baseline for W5 is fit P0 (default priors, W5 sampler settings). For each alternative prior $k$ and each stratum:

- S1: Spearman rank correlation between the vectors $\mathrm{ECI}_{m,k}$ and $\mathrm{ECI}_{m,0}$ over the stratum's models (anchors included). Pass if $\ge 0.99$.
- S2: share of the stratum's non-anchor models with relative shift $> 0.25$. Pass if $\le 5\%$, that is at most 12 of 254 (all), 1 of 20 (frontier), 3 of 72 (sparse), 9 of 182 (dense).
- S3: share of the stratum's non-anchor models with width ratio outside $[0.8, 1.25]$. Pass if $\le 5\%$ (same counts).
- S4: (a) the set of the 10 models with the highest posterior-mean ECI is the same under $k$ as under P0; (b) for each of the 9 adjacent pairs (rank $r$, rank $r+1$), $r = 1..9$, in the P0 order, $\lvert P_k(i > j) - P_0(i > j)\rvert < 0.10$. S4 is scored once and counts in the frontier and all-models strata.
- S5 (screen, no refit; the first step of W5, before any grid fit): on the draws of the W2 seed-12345 fit (its `log_prior` group), compute the log density of the three hyperpriors at each draw (sum of three LogNormal log-pdfs; the flat capability and difficulty priors are constants, and the hierarchical priors of sigma_b and log a_b are deliberately left out, following the usual advice to power-scale top-level priors only). Compute psens with the ArviZ implementation (`psens`, group "prior", default step $\alpha = 0.99$ and 1.01) for each non-anchor model's ECI draws, jointly for the three hyperpriors and separately for each. Pass in a stratum if psens $\le 0.05$ for at least 95% of its non-anchor models. Also reported, not gated: psens for $\tau_\alpha$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{median}}$ and $\tau_\sigma$ themselves, importance-reweighted ECI means at $\alpha = 0.8$ and 1.25 with the Pareto-k of the weights, and the same screen on P0's draws as a Monte Carlo check (the W2 draws decide). A hyperprior is flagged in a stratum when the pass rule above fails for its own psens. S5 is the diagnostic that guides interpretation in W5; it never replaces a grid fit.
- S5 follow-up: raising $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\mu, s)$ to the power $a$ gives $\operatorname{LogNormal}\bigl(\mu + s^2 (1 - a) / a,\ s / \sqrt{a}\bigr)$. If S5 flags a hyperprior, the two exact refits at $a = 0.8$ and $a = 1.25$ for that hyperprior are run and scored with S1-S4 like grid entries; they decide in place of the screen. These perturbations are smaller than P1-P7, so S5 is a consistency check on the refits, not an independent stress.
- S5-targeted refits (exploratory; added by the restructure, outside the C1 rule): for each flagged hyperprior, also refit at $a = 0.5$ and $a = 2$ (the same formula), at most 6 fits in all. They are scored with S1-S4 in every stratum and reported to say which prior component, and which stratum, drives any shift the grid shows. They cannot change the C1 verdict.
- PR (exploratory grid entry, M11): the W1 reference prior, fitted once at W5 settings with our reimplementation (M12) and saved with its log-likelihood, scored with S1-S4 against P0 like a grid entry and reported beside the grid. It is not in the C1 falsifier. It runs only if PR passed its W1 criterion and EQ passed.
- Noise floor: every metric is also computed for P0b (default priors, second seed) against P0. The floor must show max relative shift $\le 0.05$ and max adjacent-pair probability change $\le 0.03$; otherwise draws are doubled for all W5 fits, once.
- Reported beside S4: P(model in top 10) for each model, under each prior. In the published draws the 10th and 11th models are 0.55 points apart and the 10th has probability 0.62 of being in the top 10, so S4(a) can fail on a small shift (see Open Judgement Calls).

### M4. Simulation generators (W3)

Common to all generators. The cell list is the 2,879 (model, benchmark) rows of the fit input, with their `model_id`, `benchmark_id`, `Model`, `benchmark`, `date` and `benchmark_release_date` columns unchanged. A simulated dataset replaces only `performance`. Epoch's inclusion rule is not re-applied to simulated data. Replicate $r$ of generator $g$ uses seed = the first 4 bytes of sha256("g:r") read as a big-endian integer, for both simulation and fitting.

- G-null. Take one joint draw $\theta_r = (c, d, a, \sigma)$ from the W2 seed-12345 posterior, at position $\lfloor r \times S / 199 \rfloor$ of the pooled draws ($S$ = number of pooled draws). Compute $\mu$ and $\kappa$ as in the model and draw $y \sim \operatorname{Beta}(\mu \kappa,\ (1 - \mu) \kappa)$. The fit applies its usual clip. 199 replicates. Each replicate is also an SBC replicate (M13), because the true parameters are known and the fit returns their posterior.
- G-2D (second dimension). As G-null, then give each model a second capability $c2_m = \bar{c} + 0.7\,(c_m - \bar{c}) + \sqrt{1 - 0.49} \times s_c \times e_m$, with $e_m$ standard normal and $\bar{c}$, $s_c$ the mean and SD of $c$ over models in this draw, so that $c$ and $c2$ have correlation 0.7 and equal spread. A random 28 of the 57 benchmarks use $c2$ in place of $c$. 20 replicates.
- G-offset (developer effect). As G-null, then pick one developer at random from the seven organisations with at least 100 rows (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Alibaba, Meta AI, DeepSeek, xAI; the `Organization` column of `eci_scores.csv`) and 3 benchmarks at random from those where that developer has at least 5 rows; add 0.5 to the logit $a_b\,(c_m - d_b)$ in those cells. 20 replicates.
- G-shape (curve shape). As G-null, then for a random 19 of the 57 benchmarks multiply $\mu$ by 0.85 (an upper asymptote below the stated ceiling). 20 replicates.
- G-disp (dispersion form). As G-null for $\mu$, but $y = \mu + \sigma_b \times e$ with $e$ standard normal, clipped to $[0, 1]$, so the noise SD does not shrink toward 0 and 1. 20 replicates.
- G-drift (time drift). As G-null, then add $0.3 \times \min(3, \max(0, g_{mb}))$ to the logit, where $g_{mb}$ is the number of years from the benchmark's release date to the model's date. 20 replicates.
- G-prior (prior predictive; no fits) moved to stage W1 (M11), which uses 1,000 datasets instead of 35 and adds the reference prior.
- Positive control (real data, not simulated). Refit the real data with $\tau_\alpha \sim \operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln 0.05, 0.1)$, which forces nearly equal slopes, for seeds 12345, 22345 and 32345, and score S1-S4 against the W2 fit of the same seed.

Each simulation fit calls `fit_bayesian_eci(df_r, random_seed=seed, evaluate=False, progressbar=False)` with default priors and Epoch's default adaptive draws. A replicate is converged if Epoch's three checks pass on the returned trace. Recovery (G-null only): the true anchored value $\mathrm{ECI}^*_m = 130 + 20\,(c_m - c_{\mathrm{low}}) / (c_{\mathrm{high}} - c_{\mathrm{low}})$ from $\theta_r$ is covered if it lies within the replicate fit's [5th, 95th] percentile interval. Recovery coverage is the share covered over all converged replicates and non-anchor models, overall and per stratum, with a 90% interval from resampling replicates.

Effect sizes: the 0.7 correlation and the 0.5 logit offset are from the original plan. G-shape, G-disp and G-drift were added in review so that T1, T3 and T4 have a planted misfit; their sizes are the reviewer's choice (see Open Judgement Calls).

### M5. Check statistics T1 to T7

Each statistic is a function of a dataset and of the fit to that same dataset. Use 400 evenly spaced posterior draws. For row $i = (m, b)$, with $y_i$ the performance clipped to $[0.001, 0.999]$:

- $\mu_{is}$ and $\kappa_{bs}$ as in the model, per draw $s$; $\bar{\mu}_i$ = mean over $s$ of $\mu_{is}$;
- $v_i$ = mean over $s$ of $\mu_{is} (1 - \mu_{is}) / (\kappa_{bs} + 1)$, plus the variance over $s$ of $\mu_{is}$ (the predictive variance);
- standardised residual $z_i = (y_i - \bar{\mu}_i) / \sqrt{v_i}$;
- $\hat{c}_m$ = posterior mean of the raw capability.

Eligibility sets are fixed from the real observation pattern and reused for every simulated dataset.

- T1 (item-curve shape). For each of the 44 benchmarks with at least 20 rows: sort its rows by $\hat{c}_m$, split into 4 consecutive groups of near-equal size (`numpy.array_split`), and compute $Q_b$ = sum over groups of $n_g \times (\text{mean of } z \text{ in the group})^2$. T1 = mean of $Q_b$ over the 44 benchmarks.
- T2 (local dependence, a Q3-type statistic). For each of the 445 benchmark pairs that share at least 15 models: $r$ = Pearson correlation of $z$ over the shared models, $f = \operatorname{atanh}(r)$ with $r$ clipped to $[-0.999, 0.999]$, $q = (n - 3) f^2$. T2 = mean of $q$ over the 445 pairs. Reported beside it: max $\lvert r\rvert$ and the share of pairs with $\lvert r\rvert > 0.3$.
- T3 (dispersion form). Bin rows by $\bar{\mu}$ into $[0, 0.1)$, $[0.1, 0.3)$, $[0.3, 0.7)$, $[0.7, 0.9)$, $[0.9, 1]$; a bin with fewer than 30 rows is merged with its neighbour toward 0.5. $D_g$ = mean of $z^2$ in bin $g$. T3 = sum over bins of $(n_g / N) \times (\ln D_g)^2$.
- T4 (time drift). $\mathrm{gap}_i$ = days from `benchmark_release_date` to the model's `date` (negative allowed). T4 = $\lvert\text{Spearman correlation of } z_i \text{ and } \mathrm{gap}_i\rvert$ over all rows. Reported beside it: the Spearman correlation of $z$ with model date within each of the 44 benchmarks. (Within one benchmark the gap and the model date give the same ordering, so the original plan's two versions of this check coincide there.)
- T5 (developer x benchmark). Developer = `Organization` in `eci_scores.csv`, used verbatim; models with no organisation are left out. Among the 13 organisations with at least 20 rows, take every (organisation, benchmark) cell with at least 5 rows (185 cells). $t = (\text{sum of } z \text{ in the cell}) / \sqrt{n}$. T5 = max $\lvert t\rvert$ over cells. A developer's average residual over all benchmarks is not used, because each model's own capability absorbs it.
- T6 (boundary mass). $N_0$ = number of rows with $y \le 0.001$, $N_1$ = number with $y \ge 0.999$. $E_0$ = sum over rows of the mean over $s$ of the Beta CDF at 0.001; $E_1$ = sum over rows of the mean over $s$ of one minus the Beta CDF at 0.999. T6 = $(N_0 - E_0)^2 / (E_0 + 1) + (N_1 - E_1)^2 / (E_1 + 1)$. The per-benchmark version $c_b$ uses that benchmark's rows only.
- T7 (per-model fit). For each of the 217 models with at least 6 rows: $U_m$ = mean of $z^2$ over its rows. T7 = max of $U_m$.

All seven are non-negative and larger means worse fit. Targeted generators: T1 G-shape, T2 G-2D, T3 G-disp, T4 G-drift, T5 G-offset. T6 and T7 have none; a pass on them is labelled "power not assessed".

### M6. Calibrated p-values, family-wise adjustment, power and localisation

Reference set: the $T$ values of the $R'$ converged G-null replicates (target 199, minimum 150). Under G-null the data come from the fitted model family with parameters drawn from the W2 posterior, and each replicate is refitted, so the reference distribution includes the effect of estimating parameters from the same data. This replaces the conservative in-sample posterior predictive p-value.

- Unadjusted $p_k = (1 + \text{number of null replicates with } T_{k,r} \ge T_{k,\mathrm{obs}}) / (R' + 1)$.
- Family-wise adjustment (single-step max-T). Let $l_k = \ln(T_k + 1\mathrm{e}{-12})$. For each check $k$ compute the mean and SD of $l_k$ over the $R' + 1$ values (observed plus null) and standardise: $z_{k,j} = (l_{k,j} - \mathrm{mean}_k) / \mathrm{SD}_k$. For each null replicate $r$ let $M_r$ = max over $k$ of $z_{k,r}$. Adjusted $p_k = (1 + \text{number of } r \text{ with } M_r \ge z_{k,\mathrm{obs}}) / (R' + 1)$.
- Rejection: adjusted $p_k < 0.01$. With $R' = 199$ this happens only when $z_{k,\mathrm{obs}}$ exceeds $M_r$ for every null replicate (adjusted $p = 0.005$). With $R'$ between 150 and 198 the same holds. This controls the family-wise error rate at or below 0.01 under G-null.
- Raw in-sample $p$ (reported only): for each of the 400 draws, simulate $y_{\mathrm{rep}}$ from that draw, recompute $T_k$ on $y_{\mathrm{rep}}$ with the residual quantities ($\bar{\mu}$, $v$, $\hat{c}$) held at their real-data values, and report the share of draws with $T_k(y_{\mathrm{rep}}) \ge T_k(y)$.
- Power: a misfit replicate is evaluated exactly like the observed data (its own refit, the same reference set). Power of check $k$ against its targeted generator = share of converged replicates with adjusted $p_k < 0.01$. The requirement "power $\ge 0.8$" means at least 16 of 20, and at least 15 replicates must have converged. The 90% Clopper-Pearson interval is reported.
- Localisation (used only after a rejection or a coverage flag). Components: $Q_b$ for T1, $q$ for each pair for T2, $\lvert t\rvert$ for each cell for T5, $c_b$ for T6. A component is extreme if it exceeds the 99th percentile, over null replicates, of the largest component of that type in the replicate. $F$ is the union of: benchmarks with an extreme $Q_b$ or $c_b$; both benchmarks of an extreme pair; the benchmark of an extreme cell; and benchmarks flagged by the held-out coverage test (M7). A rejected check $k$ is localised to $F$ if, after deleting the rows of benchmarks in $F$ from the residual table (no refit) for the observed data and for every null replicate, the unadjusted $p_k$ is at least 0.01. T3, T4 and T7 have no components; they are localised only if this deletion test passes with the $F$ obtained from the other checks.
- W4d. If $F$ is non-empty and has at most 10 benchmarks: delete those rows from the fit input, re-apply Epoch's inclusion rule (at least 4 benchmarks and at least 4 mid-range per model), refit with default settings, and compute the relative shift (M1) for every model present in both fits. Models dropped by the inclusion rule are listed. If an anchor is dropped, B3d is not computable and the verdict is Inadequate.

### M7. Held-out folds and coverage (W4c)

- Eligible models: the 217 with at least 6 rows (2,704 rows). The 39 models with 4 or 5 rows (175 rows) stay in every training set and are never predicted.
- Assignment: `rng = numpy.random.default_rng(20261113)`; for each eligible model in the order of first appearance in the fit input, permute its rows with `rng.permutation` and give the row at permuted position $p$ the fold $p \bmod 5$. Every eligible row is held out exactly once, and each fold removes between $\lfloor n/5 \rfloor$ and $\lceil n/5 \rceil$ of a model's $n$ rows (about 20%). If any benchmark would have fewer than 3 training rows in some fold, redraw with seed 20261114. The assignment is written to `runs/W/W4/folds.csv` before any fold is fitted; W6 reuses it.
- Fit: for fold $f$, fit the rows not in fold $f$ with default settings and seed $12345 + f$. The inclusion rule is not re-applied.
- Prediction (M8) for each held-out row, PIT against the clipped observed score, covered if $0.05 \le \mathrm{PIT} \le 0.95$.
- Overall coverage = covered rows / 2,704, with a 90% interval from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of models. Rule: in $[0.85, 0.95]$.
- Per benchmark: for each of the 44 benchmarks with at least 20 held-out rows, an exact two-sided binomial test (`scipy.stats.binomtest`) of the covered count against 0.90. Flag if $p < 0.01 / 44$.
- Reported for comparison: Epoch's PSIS-LOO coverage (`coverage_0.9_loo`) from W4a.

### M8. How ECI predicts one score

To predict the score of model $m$ on benchmark $b$ from a fit that contains both $m$ and $b$: for each posterior draw $s$ compute $\mu_s = \operatorname{sigmoid}\bigl(a_b^{(s)} (c_m^{(s)} - d_b^{(s)})\bigr)$ clipped to $[1\mathrm{e}{-6},\ 1 - 1\mathrm{e}{-6}]$ and $\kappa_b^{(s)}$, and draw $y_{\mathrm{rep}}^{(s)} \sim \operatorname{Beta}(\mu_s \kappa,\ (1 - \mu_s) \kappa)$. Use 2,000 evenly spaced draws. The predictive mean is the mean of $\mu_s$; the 90% predictive interval is the 5th and 95th percentile of $y_{\mathrm{rep}}$.

- Target score: a value on Epoch's normalised scale, $(\text{raw as a proportion} - \mathrm{random\_baseline}_b) / (\mathrm{score\_ceiling}_b - \mathrm{random\_baseline}_b)$, clipped to $[0, 1]$ and then to $[0.001, 0.999]$. The baseline and ceiling come from `benchmark_metadata.csv`.
- PIT = share of draws with $y_{\mathrm{rep}}^{(s)} \le \text{target}$. This is Epoch's own definition (`evaluation._pit`), so results are comparable with its coverage table.
- If Epoch's own score for (m, b) is in the fit, weight the draws by that row's normalised PSIS-LOO weights (from `az.loo(trace, pointwise=True)`, as in `evaluation.loo_diagnostics`), so the prediction does not use Epoch's score for the same pair. Report the row's Pareto $k$; $k > 0.7$ marks the weights as unreliable.
- A model or benchmark that is not in the fit cannot be predicted and is excluded with a reason.

### M9. EEE crosswalk, source classes and audit (B4)

Stage a is completed and frozen before any ECI prediction is loaded.

1. Download. Fetch every `object_path` in `entries.jsonl` from the EEE datastore into `runs/B4/objects/` and verify each file's sha256 against the manifest. Failed downloads are listed.
2. Flatten. One row per `evaluation_results` element: collection, object id, `model_info.id`, `model_info.name`, `source_metadata.source_name`, `source_organization_name`, `source_type`, `evaluator_relationship`, `evaluation_name`, `source_data.dataset_name`, `metric_config` (metric id, unit, min_score, max_score, lower_is_better), `score_details.score`.
3. Benchmark map (`runs/B4/benchmark_map.csv`, by hand). A row is a candidate if the normalised text (lowercase, non-alphanumerics removed) of its collection, evaluation name or dataset name contains the normalised name or a listed alias of one of the 57 ECI benchmarks. For each distinct (collection, evaluation name, dataset name, metric id) among candidates, record the ECI benchmark, and whether the version (for example FrontierMath tier and date, Terminal-Bench 2.0, ARC-AGI 1 or 2), the split (for example semi-private or public) and the metric (the same quantity as Epoch's `score_column`) match. Include only when all three match; otherwise class version-mismatch or metric-mismatch. Record the scale that converts the EEE score to a proportion (1 if max_score is 1, 0.01 if it is 100). All 129 collections appear in `collection_screen.csv` with the number of candidate rows.
4. Model map (`runs/B4/model_map.csv`). Normalise `model_info.id` and `name` (lowercase; drop the developer prefix before "/"; replace spaces, underscores and dots with hyphens). Match against the normalised `model_version` values of `model_metadata.csv`, then against `display_name` and `model_group`. Exactly one group: mapped. More than one group, or a match that depends on a date or reasoning-setting suffix that the EEE id lacks: ambiguous, reviewed by hand and either resolved with a written reason or left as ambiguous-model. No match: model-unmapped. A group that is not among the 256 fitted models: model-not-in-fit.
5. Source class per pair (`runs/B4/source_class.csv` holds the per-source part). Compare the EEE record's source with the `source` column of Epoch's row for the same model group and benchmark:
   - same-upstream: the EEE source organisation is the organisation or leaderboard Epoch's row cites; or the EEE record relays a developer-reported score (`evaluator_relationship` is first_party, including llm-stats first-party records) and Epoch's row cites the developer's own paper or report; or the EEE source is Epoch.
   - independent: the EEE source ran the evaluation itself (`source_type` is evaluation_run, or a third-party leaderboard that runs its own evaluations) and is not the source Epoch's row cites. If Epoch's benchmark file has no `_external` suffix (Epoch ran it), any non-Epoch EEE source is independent.
   - upstream-unknown: anything else, including aggregators of unstated provenance and Epoch rows on external benchmarks with an empty `source`. Reported separately; not part of the C3 coverage.
6. Pairs. A pair is (model group, ECI benchmark, EEE source organisation). Its score is the highest normalised score among that source's records for the pair, mirroring Epoch's best-across-versions rule; the other records are class duplicate.
7. Audit. Draw 50 pairs at random (`default_rng(20261113)`) from matched-independent and matched-same-upstream pairs. For each, open the EEE JSON and Epoch's rows and check four things: (i) the EEE model is a version that `model_metadata.csv` lists under the mapped group; (ii) benchmark, version and split are the ones Epoch uses; (iii) metric and scale are right and the normalised score recomputed by hand equals the stored one; (iv) the source class follows rule 5. A pair with any failed item is one error. Results go to `runs/B4/audit_sample.csv` with one column per item. Pass: at most 2 errors in 50. The Clopper-Pearson 95% upper bound on the error rate is reported (5.8% for 0 errors, 12.1% for 2). On failure: fix the cause, rebuild, and audit a fresh sample of 50 drawn with seed 20261114; a second failure stops B4. The audit is done by the analyst who built the maps, so it is a self-check.
8. Freeze. Record the sha256 of the three map files in `runs/freeze.json`. Stage b refuses to run if they differ.

Stage b:

- Same-upstream control: for matched-same-upstream pairs, $\lvert\text{EEE normalised score} - \text{Epoch's normalised score in the fit input}\rvert$. Requirement: median $\le 0.02$.
- Coverage: PIT (M8) for every matched-independent pair; coverage = share with $0.05 \le \mathrm{PIT} \le 0.95$; 90% interval from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of model groups. Reported also by benchmark, by whether Epoch's own score for the pair is in the fit, and with two sensitivity cuts (pairs with Pareto $k > 0.7$ removed; pairs whose raw score equals Epoch's to 3 decimals removed).
- Verdict label: `harness-limited` is the registered name of the C3 verdict given when independent sources disagree with each other more than the model's noise allows (agreement below 0.80). It means the comparison is limited by differences in evaluation setup between sources, not by ECI.
- EEE-vs-EEE agreement: for every (model group, benchmark) with at least 2 independent sources, each unordered pair of sources agrees if the absolute difference of their normalised scores is at most $\sqrt{2} \times (q_{95} - q_{05}) / 2$, where $q_{05}$ and $q_{95}$ are the unweighted ECI predictive quantiles for that model and benchmark. If the two scores were independent draws from the ECI predictive distribution, about 90% would agree. Agreement is estimable with at least 30 source pairs.
- Bias: mean of $(\text{EEE normalised score} - \text{ECI predictive mean})$ per benchmark. Epoch's best-of rule predicts a negative sign for sources that report a single run.

### M11. Prior predictive check and the reference prior (W1)

- Cells and simulation. The cell list is the 2,879 (model, benchmark) rows of the fit input; no score is read. For each of $D = 1{,}000$ datasets, draw one complete parameter set from the prior, compute $\mu$ and $\kappa$ as in the model, and draw $y \sim \operatorname{Beta}(\mu\kappa,\ (1 - \mu)\kappa)$. Two priors are simulated, each from its own stream of `numpy.random.SeedSequence(20261113).spawn(2)`: Epoch's (difficulties drawn uniform on $[-50, 50]$ and centred to sum to 0; $\sigma_b$ redrawn until below 0.5, as the truncation requires) and PR.
- Reported per prior. For each dataset: the share of scores at the clip bounds ($y \le 0.001$ or $y \ge 0.999$, which the fit clips) and the share in $[0.1, 0.9]$; over datasets, the mean and the 5th and 95th percentiles of each. Also the share of cells whose pre-noise $\mu$ lies in $[0.1, 0.9]$ and the share with $\lvert a_b (c_m - d_b)\rvert > 6.9$ (where $\mu$ is within 0.001 of 0 or 1). Epoch's priors should reproduce the review's figures, 0.89 and 0.05, to within 0.01; if they do not, the calculation is repeated before anything else is read.
- Reference prior PR. On the raw scale the geometric-mean slope is 1, so $a_b (c_m - d_b)$ is a logit and a gap above about 7 saturates a score at the clip bound (the book's Ch. 4 sets a $\operatorname{Normal}(0, 5)$ prior on an average logit, which confines the probability to about $(0.007, 0.993)$). PR replaces only the flat priors on capability and difficulty:

```math
\begin{aligned}
s &\sim \operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln 2,\ 0.5), \qquad \mu_c \sim \operatorname{Normal}(0,\ 2),\\
c_m &\sim \operatorname{Normal}(\mu_c,\ s), \qquad d_b \sim \operatorname{ZeroSumNormal}\bigl(s\sqrt{K / (K - 1)}\bigr), \quad K = 57,
\end{aligned}
```

  with $\tau_\alpha$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{median}}$, $\tau_\sigma$, the log slopes and $\sigma_b$ as in Epoch's model. A shared hierarchical scale $s$ (median 2, 95% interval 0.75 to 5.3) keeps the prior weak for the weakest models, whose published raw capability means run to $-8.9$, without fixing the scale by hand (Open Judgement Call 3 on P8). PR is exploratory; it adds an entry to the W5 grid and a candidate in W6, and replaces Epoch's prior in no verdict.
- Plausibility criterion (pre-declared; all three must hold): (i) the mean over datasets of the share in $[0.1, 0.9]$ is at least 0.40; (ii) at least 950 of the 1,000 datasets have that share at or above 0.15; (iii) the mean share at the clip bounds is at most 0.35. Reason: Epoch's inclusion rule keeps only models with at least 4 mid-range scores on at least 4 benchmarks, so a generator in which mid-range scores are rare cannot produce the data the pipeline admits. The bound share is allowed to be large because the observed pattern is selective (models are run on benchmarks near their level, so random pairs of $c$ and $d$ overstate saturation; the fit input has 81 of 2,879 scores, 2.8%, exactly 0 or 1).
- Attainability (checked on the observation pattern before registration): PR gives mean mid-range share 0.45, 98.5% of datasets at or above 0.15 and mean bound share 0.26; Epoch's priors give 0.05, 0.2% and 0.89, so they fail all three. The check used no scores or fits, but the published capability spread from the review was in view when the scale of $s$ was set (Open Judgement Call 12).
- Outcome. No gate stops on W1. If PR passes, it is used in W5 and W6; if it fails, it is dropped from both, the failure is reported, and PR is not retuned. The result for Epoch's priors is descriptive: it says the flat priors are not generative, not that the fit is wrong, because the data dominate them.
- J1 and J2 (normalised Jeffreys priors on capability, exploratory, added post-freeze; Revision Log R26). Added at the user's request as robustness arms; they enter no gate and no confirmatory verdict (C1 to C3 unchanged). Each keeps everything in Epoch's model (the hyperpriors, the slopes, the noise, the flat zero-sum box prior on $d$ and the box on $c$) and adds one potential, the Jeffreys prior on each capability conditional on the slopes, difficulties and noise, normalised over the box:

```math
\sum_m \Bigl[ \tfrac12 \log I_m(c_m) - \log Z_m \Bigr], \qquad
I_m(c) = \sum_{b \in \mathrm{obs}(m)} a_b^2\, g\bigl(a_b (c - d_b),\, \kappa_b\bigr), \qquad
Z_m = \int_{-50}^{50} \sqrt{I_m(c)}\, dc,
```

  so $c_m$ has the proper density $\sqrt{I_m(c)} / Z_m$ on $[-50, 50]$, the sums run over the observed cells, and the prior marginal of everything except the capabilities is exactly Epoch's (the factor integrates to one over $c$), so it cannot pressure the slopes. Difficulties get no Jeffreys term. The two variants differ in $g(\eta, \kappa)$, the information about $\eta$ of one observation, with $\mu = \operatorname{sigmoid}(\eta)$:

```math
\text{J1 (Beta information):}\ \ g = [\mu(1-\mu)]^2\, \kappa^2\, \bigl[\psi_1(\mu\kappa) + \psi_1((1-\mu)\kappa)\bigr], \qquad
\text{J2 (Bernoulli information):}\ \ g = \mu(1-\mu).
```

  Here $\psi_1$ is the trigamma function. For J1, $\kappa^2[\psi_1(\mu\kappa) + \psi_1((1-\mu)\kappa)]$ is the Fisher information about $\mu$ of one $\operatorname{Beta}(\mu\kappa, (1-\mu)\kappa)$ observation and $d\mu/d\eta = \mu(1-\mu)$ turns it into the information about $\eta$. J2 is the textbook item-response form and is Jeffreys only heuristically for the Beta likelihood. Under a Jeffreys ability prior the posterior mode is the weighted likelihood estimate (Warm, 1989, Psychometrika 54, 427-450). Neither prior accounts for the clipping of $y$ to $[10^{-3}, 1 - 10^{-3}]$ in the likelihood, and both ignore the cross terms between $c_m$ and $d_b$. A full joint Jeffreys prior was not used: the joint information matrix of $(c, d)$ is singular along the common shift of $c$ and $d$, so the prior is improper there; it is not defined over the hierarchical hyperpriors; and the item-response literature uses the ability-conditional form.
- J1 and J2, first form J (rejected). The first form added the unnormalised $\tfrac12\sum_m\log I_m + \tfrac12\sum_b\log I_b$ on both capabilities and difficulties. It is not normalisable in the slope scale (with $\log a = \tau_\alpha w$ fixed, its log density in $\tau_\alpha$ rises from 437 at 0.25 to 15,280 at 32, because the information tends to 1 when $\eta$ saturates and the root-mean-square slope exceeds the geometric mean), and NUTS on it fails with slopes near $e^{358}$. The evidence is in `results/W/W1J/summary_J_first_form.md`; the user chose J1 and J2 instead.
- J1 and J2, numerics. $\log\mu(1-\mu) = -\operatorname{softplus}(-\eta) - \operatorname{softplus}(\eta)$ is taken from the unclipped $\eta$, because the clip on $\mu$ has zero gradient. For J1, $g = (1-\mu)^2 h(\mu\kappa) + \mu^2 h((1-\mu)\kappa)$ with $h(x) = x^2\psi_1(x) = 1 + x^2\psi_1(x+1)$, which stays finite as $\mu \to 0$ or $1$; $\psi_1$ is evaluated by recurrence up to an argument of at least 7 and then the asymptotic series, differentiably (relative error against `scipy.special.polygamma` at most $2\times10^{-14}$; PyTensor's `tri_gamma` has a working gradient but runs in Python object mode and is about 100 times slower). $Z_m$ is a fixed-node composite Gauss-Legendre rule (304 nodes: unit panels of 8 nodes on $[-16, 16]$, coarse panels outside), differentiable in PyTensor, accurate to better than $10^{-6}$ against `scipy.integrate.quad` for difficulties within about $\pm 16$ and slopes up to about 6 (the W2 posterior means of $d$ span $-9.5$ to $3.9$). One log density with gradient costs about 41 times F0's for J1 and 9 times for J2. Builder: `runs/lib/jeffreys_norm.py`; with the potential removed it equals F0 exactly.
- J1 and J2, W1 and W5. W1 reports for each the same quantities as for the other priors, by direct simulation: hyperparameters, slopes and noise from Epoch's priors, difficulties as centred uniforms exactly as in W1's Epoch arm, then each $c_m$ from $\sqrt{I_m(c)}/Z_m$ by inverse CDF on a grid of 8,001 points; descriptive for J1 and J2. Each is fitted once at W5 settings like PR (the PR entry's seed, draws and sampler call), saved with its log-likelihood, scored with S1-S4 against P0 and reported beside PR; neither is in the C1 falsifier, and both go to W5 whatever W1 shows, because the aim is robustness. Results: `results/W/W1J/summary.md`.

### M12. Our implementation of the base model and the equivalence check EQ (W2)

- Epoch's code is never edited. Expansions need a model Epoch's builder does not offer, so `runs/lib/models.py` builds its own PyMC models: F0 has the same variables, parametrisation and priors as `build_bayesian_model`; F1 to F7 and PR are in M14 and M11. Our models are sampled by our own call: NUTS, 4 chains, target acceptance 0.95, 1,000 tuning steps and 1,000 draws per chain, draws doubled up to 4,000 until Epoch's three convergence checks pass, and, if divergences remain at 4,000, one rerun at target acceptance 0.99. It does not use Epoch's pilot with a dense mass matrix, which looks up variables by names a different model does not have.
- Equivalence check EQ, pre-declared; every item must hold:
  1. Density: at 20 evenly spaced draws of the W2 seed-12345 trace (all free variables), the joint log density of F0 equals that of Epoch's model to within $1\mathrm{e}{-6}$ in absolute value.
  2. Fit: F0 fitted to the fit input with seeds 12345 and 22345, compared with the W2 fit of the same seed, meets for each seed $\lvert\Delta\mathrm{ECI}_m\rvert \le \max(0.5,\ 0.07 \times W_m)$ for every non-anchor model, with $W_m$ the width in the W2 fit; Spearman $\ge 0.999$ over 256 models; median width ratio in $[0.95, 1.05]$ and at least 95% of the 254 ratios in $[0.85, 1.15]$; and zero divergences, $\mathrm{r\_hat} \le 1.01$, bulk ESS $\ge 400$.
  3. Prediction: $\lvert\widehat{\mathrm{elpd}}_{\mathrm{loo}}(\mathrm{F0}) - \widehat{\mathrm{elpd}}_{\mathrm{loo}}(\mathrm{Epoch})\rvert \le 2$ for the same seed (PSIS-LOO over the 2,879 rows). The limit is half the W6 decision threshold of 4, so a reimplementation difference cannot pass for an expansion effect.
- The item 2 tolerance is G0's, applied to our model against our own run of Epoch's code, so only Monte Carlo error is expected to separate them. If EQ fails, the cause is found and fixed once and EQ is rerun; a second failure means PR (in W5) and W6 are not run and the failure is reported. Nothing else changes.
- The F0 fit of seed 12345 is saved with its log-likelihood and is the baseline for W6.

### M13. Simulation-based calibration conditional on the observed data (W3)

- Replicates. The 199 G-null replicates (M4), refitted by Epoch's code with defaults. For replicate $r$ the true parameters $\theta_r$ are one joint draw from the W2 seed-12345 posterior, and the fit returns 400 evenly spaced posterior draws.
- Rank statistic. For parameter $j$ with true value $\theta^*_{rj}$ and fitted draws $\theta^{(1)}_j, \dots, \theta^{(L)}_j$, $L = 400$: $u_{rj} = \bigl(\#\{\ell : \theta^{(\ell)}_j < \theta^*_{rj}\} + 0.5\bigr) / L$. If the fit is calibrated, $u_{rj}$ is uniform on $(0, 1)$. Tested parameters $j$: the anchored ECI of the 254 non-anchor models and the EDI of the 57 benchmarks (311 in all), with true values anchored by $\theta_r$'s own anchor draws.
- Test 1 (per parameter). Pearson $\chi^2$ of the converged replicates' $u_{rj}$ over 10 equal-width bins against uniform (9 degrees of freedom; about 20 expected per bin at 199 replicates). Parameter $j$ is flagged if $p < 0.01 / 311$ (Bonferroni, family-wise 0.01 as in M6).
- Test 2 (pooled coverage, per stratum). Over replicates and the stratum's non-anchor models, the share of $u_{rj}$ in $[0.25, 0.75]$ (central 50%) and in $[0.05, 0.95]$ (central 90%), with a 90% interval from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of replicates, for the all-models, frontier and sparse strata. The central 90% share over all non-anchor models is exactly the recovery coverage of G1 (M4), which is unchanged.
- Descriptive only: rank histograms of $\tau_\alpha$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{median}}$, $\tau_\sigma$ and the per-benchmark slopes and $\sigma_b$. No test, because their priors are informative and the truth was drawn from the posterior, so uniform ranks are not expected.
- SBC verdict. Pass if no parameter is flagged by Test 1, the central 90% share is in $[0.85, 0.95]$ in the all-models and frontier strata, and the central 50% share is in $[0.45, 0.55]$ in the all-models stratum. For Epoch's model the verdict is reported and exploratory and changes no G1 branch. It tells which of sampler, model and observed-data conditioning to suspect when recovery fails.
- What conditioning on the data changes. Textbook SBC draws the true parameters from the prior used in the fit, which makes the ranks exactly uniform. Here the draws come from the fitted posterior, because draws from the $\operatorname{Uniform}(-50, 50)$ priors give data with 89% of scores at the clip bounds (W1) and say nothing about the data actually fitted. For capability and difficulty the fitted prior is flat over a range far wider than the posterior, so a posterior draw is close to a draw from the prior restricted to the region the data support, and near-uniform ranks are expected; for parameters with informative priors they are not. A pass therefore means calibrated near the observed fit, not calibrated everywhere (see Risks).
- Reduced SBC for an expansion (M14). 30 replicates (20 for F1), drawn from the candidate's own fitted posterior and refitted with the candidate; the rank statistic is as above, with ECI of the 254 non-anchor models taken from the candidate's capability $c_m$. Only Test 2 is applied (30 ranks per parameter cannot support a 10-bin test): central 90% share in $[0.85, 0.95]$ in the all-models and frontier strata and central 50% share in $[0.45, 0.55]$ in the all-models stratum. The reduced check can miss a calibration fault that the full one would find.

### M14. Model expansion and comparison (W6, exploratory)

The candidates and what each mirrors. Every expansion contains F0 as a special case (the added effect is zero), and every parameter it shares with F0 keeps Epoch's prior, so a difference comes from the added term. The priors on added parameters are weak on the logit scale. Scores are modelled as before with $\mu_{mb}$ the Beta mean, $\eta_{mb}$ the logit of $\mu_{mb}$ before the clip, and $g_{mb} = \min(3, \max(0, \text{years from the benchmark's release to the model's date}))$.

| ID | Model | Change to $\eta_{mb} = a_b (c_m - d_b)$ | Mirrors | Added parameters and priors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F0 | base, Epoch's priors | none (our F0 from EQ) | none | none |
| F1 | two-dimensional capability | $\eta_{mb} = a_b (c_m + \lambda_b z_m - d_b)$ | G-2D, T2 | $z_m \sim \operatorname{Normal}(0, 1)$ for 256 models; $\lambda_b = \tau_\lambda \xi_b$, $\xi_b \sim \operatorname{Normal}(0, 1)$, $\tau_\lambda \sim \operatorname{HalfNormal}(1)$; the sign is fixed by constraining the loading of the benchmark with the most rows to be positive |
| F2 | developer-by-benchmark offset | add $\delta_{o(m), b}$ on the 185 cells of T5 (developer $o(m)$), zero elsewhere | G-offset, T5 | $\delta = \tau_\delta \zeta$, $\zeta \sim \operatorname{Normal}(0, 1)$, $\tau_\delta \sim \operatorname{HalfNormal}(0.5)$ |
| F3 | upper asymptote below 1 | $\mu_{mb} = (1 - \epsilon_b)\operatorname{sigmoid}(\eta_{mb})$ | G-shape, T1 | $\epsilon_b \sim \operatorname{Beta}(1, 9)$ per benchmark (mean 0.1, 95% below 0.26) |
| F4 | time drift | add $\gamma\, g_{mb}$ | G-drift, T4 | $\gamma \sim \operatorname{Normal}(0, 0.5^2)$ logit per year |
| F5 | per-model noise scale | $\sigma_{mb} = 0.5\operatorname{sigmoid}\bigl(\operatorname{logit}(2\sigma_b) + \omega_m\bigr)$ in place of $\sigma_b$ | G-disp, T3, T7 | $\omega_m = \tau_\omega \varphi_m$, $\varphi_m \sim \operatorname{Normal}(0, 1)$, $\tau_\omega \sim \operatorname{HalfNormal}(0.5)$ |
| F6 | base under the reference prior | PR (M11) | W1 | PR's $s$ and $\mu_c$ |
| F7 | combination | only if M14's iteration rule triggers | none | those of the combined features |

F3 is the analogue of the book's "allow for guessing" step (Ch. 4, a lower asymptote of 0.25 for four-option items): Epoch's scores are normalised to chance, so the departure to allow for is a ceiling below 1. F5 cannot reproduce G-disp's constant-SD noise, which a Beta likelihood cannot represent near the bounds; it tests whether noise varies by model.

Computational check. Each fit follows M12's sampler and must pass Epoch's three convergence checks on every parameter group, including the added ones.

Comparison.

- Primary estimator: PSIS-LOO elpd over all 2,879 rows (ArviZ `loo`, pointwise). For candidate $j$: $\Delta_j = \widehat{\mathrm{elpd}}_j - \widehat{\mathrm{elpd}}_0$ and $\mathrm{SE}_j = \sqrt{n}$ times the SD over rows of the pointwise elpd differences.
- K-fold trigger. If, for either model of a pair (F0, $F_j$), more than 2% of the rows (more than 57 of 2,879) have Pareto $\hat{k} > 0.7$, PSIS-LOO is replaced for that pair by 5-fold cross-validation on the M7 folds (the same assignment as W4c), summed over the 2,704 eligible rows only, for both models. $\Delta_j$ and $\mathrm{SE}_j$ are then computed from the held-out row-wise log predictive density. F0's fold fits are those of W4c (Epoch's code, EQ having shown the two implementations equivalent); each $F_j$ is refitted on the same folds with seed $12345 + f$, and the inclusion rule is not re-applied. Reason: the book (Ch. 24) names K-fold with refitting the robust alternative when PSIS-LOO shows many high $\hat{k}$. The 2% share is where 57 rows with an average error of about 0.07 log units each could equal the decision threshold of 4. Sparse models, whose capability rests on 4 to 6 scores, are expected to trigger it, so the plan budgets for K-fold (Open Judgement Call 14).
- Preference rule. $F_j$ ($j = 1, \dots, 6$) is preferred over F0 if $\Delta_j > 4$ and $\Delta_j > 2.5\,\mathrm{SE}_j$. Rationale: below 4 the standard error is likely underestimated and the difference is skewed, so a normal approximation is unreliable (Ch. 17); 2.5 standard errors, one-sided about 0.006, is stricter than the common 2 because six candidates are compared with the same base (family-wise about 0.04).
- Adoption. A preferred expansion is adopted only if its computational checks pass and its reduced SBC (M13) passes. SBC is run only for preferred expansions, since an expansion that is not preferred cannot enter the adequate set. A preferred expansion that fails either is reported as "preferred, not adopted".
- Output. If nothing is adopted: "base model adequate". Otherwise a set $A$ of adequate models: the adopted expansions whose elpd lies within 4 of the best adopted expansion's elpd (F0 is not in $A$). ECI is reported under F0 and under every model in $A$ (a small multiverse; for F1 to F5 the ECI is built from $c_m$ with the same anchors), with the S1-S4 metrics of M3, the S2 and S3 shares, $P(\text{in top 10})$ and the per-model shifts in the all-models, frontier and sparse strata relative to F0 refitted at W6 settings. These are descriptive: the C1 thresholds are used only to label each as would-pass or would-fail, and no verdict follows.
- Iteration, pre-declared to avoid forking paths. Allowed: (I1) a sampler failure follows the fallback below, once per step; (I2) if two or more different single-feature expansions among F1 to F5 are adopted, one combined model F7 containing the features of the (at most three) adopted expansions with the largest $\Delta_j$ is fitted and compared with the best adopted single expansion by the same preference rule and adoption conditions; F7 enters $A$ if preferred and adopted; there is no further combination. Not allowed: candidates beyond F0 to F7; changes to a prior, functional form, threshold, K or fold assignment after any elpd is seen; extra seeds chosen to improve an elpd; dropping a candidate; a second round. F6 is not combined, since it changes the prior and not the structure. At most 8 candidates are ever fitted.
- Fallback for F1 (the slowest). F1 has failed if, after draws of 1,000, 2,000 and 4,000 per chain, there is any divergence, $\mathrm{r\_hat} > 1.01$ or bulk ESS $< 400$, or one fit exceeds 60 minutes. Then, in order, each used once: (1) rerun at target acceptance 0.99; (2) restrict the second dimension to the 44 benchmarks with at least 20 rows ($\lambda_b = 0$ for the other 13); (3) if that also fails, report F1 as "not fitted" and leave it out of $A$. No variational approximation is substituted, because its elpd is not comparable. The two-dimensional question is then answered by T2 in W4 alone, as a descriptive result.
- Compute estimates (4 CPUs, one fit at a time; per-fit times are our estimates from Epoch's quoted two minutes scaled by parameter count, to be replaced by the time measured on the EQ fit before W6 starts):

| Item | Per fit | Count | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Candidate fits on the real data (F1 to F5; F6 reuses W5's fit) | F1 20 min, F2 6, F3 3, F4 3, F5 6 | 5 | about 0.6 h (1.3 h with every retry) |
| K-fold refits if triggered (5 folds at about 0.8 of a full fit; F0's come from W4) | F1 16 min, F2 5, F3 2.5, F4 2.5, F5 5, F6 2.5 | 30 | about 2.7 h (5.5 h with every retry) |
| Reduced SBC, preferred expansions only (30 replicates, 20 for F1) | as the per-fit times above | 0 to 6 models | 1.5 to 3 h per preferred expansion except F1 (6.7 h); typically 1 or 2 are preferred |
| Combination F7, only if two or more are adopted | about 12 min for a fit without F1 | 1 fit, 5 folds, 30 replicates | up to 8 h |

  W6 totals about 3 to 10 h typically (none or up to two expansions preferred, K-fold triggered). The worst case, with every candidate triggering K-fold, being preferred and needing SBC, is about 24 h, plus up to 8 h for F7. W6 is restartable per fit, and may be stopped between candidates without affecting C1 to C3.

## Risks and Confounds

- Risk: the published file comes from Epoch's production pipeline, which may differ from the public defaults. The public loader already selects different model versions for three GPQA diamond rows. Mitigation: W2 fits the published fit input, reports the loader-input fit as W2b, and G0 decides whether to rebaseline.
- Risk: Monte Carlo error is mistaken for prior sensitivity or for a reproduction failure. Mitigation: 4,000 draws per chain in W5, a second-seed noise floor for every metric, and a G0 tolerance scaled to interval width.
- Risk: flat priors could let sparse models drift toward the $\pm 50$ bounds. The published draws stay within abs 12.6 on the raw scale, so this is not expected; W5 still reports posterior mass beyond abs 49.
- Risk: the anchor transform (Claude 3.5 Sonnet = 130, GPT-5 = 150) moves every score when a prior moves either anchor. Mitigation: S1 and S4 are scale-free; S2 and S3 are also reported on the raw scale; B6 swaps the anchors.
- Risk: in-sample posterior predictive p-values are conservative. Mitigation: p-values come from refitted null simulations (M6).
- Risk: the null simulations use parameters drawn from the posterior of the same data the checks are applied to. This is a parametric bootstrap and is valid for testing the model family, but the reference distribution is conditional on the fitted parameter region; recovery and power results hold for that region only. The SBC (M13) has the same conditioning, so a pass means calibrated near the observed fit.
- Risk: our reimplementation of the base model differs from Epoch's in a way that makes an expansion look better or worse than it is. Mitigation: EQ (M12) before any expansion is fitted (density equality, the G0 tolerance on the fit, elpd within 2), and F0, our own fit through the same sampler call, is the comparator in W6, not Epoch's fit.
- Risk: forking paths in W6 (six candidates, one base, several looks at elpd). Mitigation: the candidate list, rule, thresholds, K-fold trigger and the only two allowed iterations are fixed in M14 before any fit; every candidate is reported; the preference rule uses 2.5 standard errors; the C1 and C2 verdict files are digested before any W6 elpd is computed.
- Risk: PSIS-LOO is unreliable for sparse models, whose capability rests on 4 to 6 scores. Mitigation: the K-fold trigger (more than 2% of rows with $\hat{k} > 0.7$) and K-fold on the M7 folds; the K-fold elpd covers the 2,704 eligible rows only.
- Risk: elpd measures prediction, not ranking, so an expansion that is not preferred can still move scores. Mitigation: its elpd is listed, but ECI under it is summarised only if it is adequate; the descriptive S1-S4 comparison covers adequate models only.
- Risk: the reduced SBC for an expansion (30 replicates, coverage only) can miss a calibration fault. Mitigation: the fault would have to survive the W2-style convergence checks and a 0.05 coverage band; the limitation is stated wherever the adequate set is reported.
- Risk: checks without a planted misfit (T6, T7) or with low measured power pass uninformatively. Mitigation: they are labelled and cannot support "Adequate".
- Risk: EEE and Epoch share upstreams, so a match there is transcription, not replication. Mitigation: per-pair source class (M9); same-upstream pairs are used only as the positive control.
- Risk: Epoch keeps the best score across a model group's versions, so single-run independent scores are expected to be lower. Mitigation: signed bias per benchmark is reported and read together with coverage.
- Risk: model-name crosswalk errors. Mitigation: a four-item audit of 50 pairs with at most 2 errors, and every ambiguous case excluded with a reason.
- Risk: benchmark version drift (FrontierMath tiers and dates, Terminal-Bench versions, ARC-AGI splits). Mitigation: versions are mapped by hand and mismatches excluded before scoring.
- Risk: Epoch's data change daily. Mitigation: the 2026-11-13 zip is pinned by sha256, and every result names it.

## Open Judgement Calls

These were left for the plan owner. The plan runs as written if none is changed.

1. Baseline data. The plan now treats the published fit input as the baseline and the loader output as a reported variant (W2b). The opposite choice is defensible if the aim is "what the public code produces today". The three differing rows include the top-ranked model.
2. S4(a), "top-10 set unchanged". In the published draws the 10th model has probability 0.62 of being in the top 10 and leads the 11th by 0.55 points, so a shift well inside the S2 tolerance can fail S4(a) and falsify C1. The rule is kept as registered; $P(\text{in top 10})$ is reported so a reader can judge. A probability-based version (no model's $P(\text{in top 10})$ changes by 0.10 or more) would be more stable.
3. P8, $\operatorname{Normal}(0, 3)$ on raw capability. The published raw capability means run from -8.9 to 3.2, so the weakest models sit about 3 prior SDs from the prior centre and P8 is informative for them. It is kept as a defensible proper alternative; if the owner sees it as a stress prior it should move to the X list before any fit. The guard that rescales P8 when the spread exceeds 3 is discontinuous at 3.
4. Sizes of the added misfit generators (0.85 asymptote on a third of benchmarks; constant-SD noise; 0.3 logit per year). They set what "power" means for T1, T3 and T4.
5. The family-wise test rejects only when the observed statistic exceeds all 199 null replicates. More null replicates (999) would give finer p-values at about five times the W3 cost.
6. C3 verdicts use point estimates. With few independent pairs the bootstrap interval may span two verdict bands; the minimum of 100 pairs is the reviewer's choice.
7. The audit rule (at most 2 errors in 50) leaves a 95% upper bound of 12% on the true error rate. A sample of 100 with at most 2 errors would bound it at 6.2%.
8. EEE datastore location (resolved 2026-11-13 by the plan owner): objects come from the Hugging Face dataset `evaleval/EEE_datastore` at each `object_path` in manifest dd7efcff; every download is verified against its manifest sha256, so the pin is by content.

Added by the restructure of 2026-11-18, for the plan owner to confirm or change before any fit:

9. S5 source. The screen now runs on the W2 seed-12345 draws (first in W5) and not on P0's 4,000-draw refit; the threshold ($\mathrm{psens} \le 0.05$ for 95% of models) is unchanged. The W2 draws are fewer, so the Monte Carlo error of psens is larger; the P0 screen is reported beside it and does not decide. The restructure also states what "flagged" means (the pass rule applied to a hyperprior's own psens), which the first version left implicit.
10. S5-targeted refits. Powers 0.5 and 2 (stronger than the C1 follow-up's 0.8 and 1.25) and a cap of 6 fits are the author's choice. They are exploratory and cannot move the C1 verdict.
11. EQ. The tolerance is G0's, applied to our model against our own run of Epoch's code on two seeds, with elpd within 2 (half the W6 threshold). A tighter fit tolerance is plausible, since the two runs share a seed and a model; it was not set tighter because the samplers differ (Epoch's dense-mass-matrix pilot against ours).
12. Reference prior PR (M11). $s \sim \operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln 2, 0.5)$ shared by capability and difficulty, $\mu_c \sim \operatorname{Normal}(0, 2)$, and the criterion (mean mid-range share at least 0.40; 950 of 1,000 datasets at or above 0.15; mean clip-bound share at most 0.35) were set by prior predictive simulation on the observation pattern, with the published capability spread (raw means from $-8.9$ to 3.2) in view when the scale was chosen. A fixed-scale prior (P8 is one), a Student-t prior or a different bound share would be defensible; the clip-bound limit of 0.35 is generous because the observed pattern is selective (2.8% of the real scores are exactly 0 or 1). PR is exploratory, so none of this touches a verdict.
13. W6 rule. Preference at an elpd difference above 4 and above 2.5 standard errors (the common heuristic is 4 and 2); the adequate set as the adopted expansions within 4 of the best; one combination round; the candidate forms and weak priors in M14 (for example the $\operatorname{Beta}(1, 9)$ prior on the asymptote gap and the $\operatorname{HalfNormal}(0.5)$ scales), and F5's per-model noise scale as the dispersion candidate, because Beta noise cannot be made constant-SD. Stricter or looser values change which expansions are adopted, not any registered verdict.
14. K-fold trigger. More than 2% of rows (57 of 2,879) with Pareto $\hat{k} > 0.7$ in either model of a pair switches that pair to the M7 five folds. The share comes from 57 rows at an average error of about 0.07 matching the threshold of 4, and is a judgement. It is expected to trigger because sparse models rest on 4 to 6 scores, so the compute estimates budget for K-fold. The M7 folds hold out only the 217 models with at least 6 rows, so a K-fold comparison says nothing about the 39 sparsest models.
15. SBC rules (M13). 10 bins and a Bonferroni level of $0.01 / 311$ for the per-parameter test; bands $[0.85, 0.95]$ for the central 90% share and $[0.45, 0.55]$ for the central 50% share; 30 replicates (20 for F1) for the reduced check. The full check adds nothing to any gate for Epoch's model, so a stricter reading changes no verdict; for an expansion the bands decide adoption.
