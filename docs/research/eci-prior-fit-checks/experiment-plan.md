## Orientation for a New Reader

This section explains the objects the plan refers to. Every statement about Epoch's code was checked against `ext/eci-public` at commit e04929a, and every count against the data release in `ext/epoch_zip/benchmark_data.zip`.

**Epoch AI and the ECI.** Epoch AI is a research organisation that collects benchmark scores for AI language models. Its Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) summarises each model's scores on many benchmarks as one number. A model that scores higher on harder benchmarks gets a higher ECI. The companion number for a benchmark is its difficulty index (EDI).

**The data the model is fitted to.** The fit input has one row per (model, benchmark) pair with a `performance` value in $[0, 1]$. A raw score is normalised as $(\mathrm{raw} \times \mathrm{scale} - \mathrm{random\_baseline}) / (\mathrm{score\_ceiling} - \mathrm{random\_baseline})$ and clipped to $[0, 1]$, so 0 means chance level and 1 means the benchmark's stated ceiling; the baseline and ceiling per benchmark are in `benchmark_metadata.csv`. A "model" is a model group: `model_metadata.csv` maps 1,227 model versions to groups, and the group's score on a benchmark is the best score of any of its versions. Epoch keeps rows dated 2023-01-01 or later, drops a benchmark's older variant where a group also has the newer one, and keeps a model only if it has at least 4 benchmarks and at least 4 benchmarks with a score in $[0.1, 0.9]$ (called mid-range in this plan). The published fit input, `epoch_capabilities_index/processed_data_for_eci.csv`, has 2,879 rows for 256 models and 57 benchmarks (58 benchmarks are flagged `in_eci`; SuperGLUE has no qualifying rows). Rows per model range from 4 to 29 (median 10); 72 models have 6 or fewer. 59 scores are exactly 0 and 22 are exactly 1.

**The model.** For model $m$ with capability $c_m$ and benchmark $b$ with difficulty $d_b$, slope $a_b > 0$ and noise level $\sigma_b$:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{mb} &= \operatorname{sigmoid}\bigl(a_b \times (c_m - d_b)\bigr), \quad \text{clipped to } [1\mathrm{e}{-6},\ 1 - 1\mathrm{e}{-6}];\\
\kappa_b &= \frac{1}{4\sigma_b^2} - 1;\\
y_{mb} &\sim \operatorname{Beta}\bigl(\mu_{mb} \times \kappa_b,\ (1 - \mu_{mb}) \times \kappa_b\bigr),
\end{aligned}
```

where $y$ is the performance clipped to $[0.001, 0.999]$ (`performance_clip_eps = 1e-3`).

The Beta's standard deviation is $2\sqrt{\mu (1 - \mu)}\,\sigma_b$, so $\sigma_b$ is the noise SD where the curve passes 0.5 and the noise shrinks toward 0 and 1. This is a one-dimensional item response model with a Beta likelihood.

**The priors** (class `BayesianPriors` and function `build_bayesian_model` in `src/eci/bayesian.py`; "$\operatorname{LogNormal}(m, s)$" has log-mean $m$ and log-SD $s$):

| Parameter | Prior | Comment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $c_m$ (capability) | $\operatorname{Uniform}(-50, 50)$ | flat; not configurable |
| $d_b$ (difficulty) | flat on the subspace where the $d_b$ sum to 0, each bounded to $[-50, 50]$ | the sum fixes the location of the scale |
| $\log a_b$ (log slope) | ZeroSumNormal with SD $\tau_\alpha \times \sqrt{K / (K - 1)}$, $K = 57$ | log slopes sum to 0, so the geometric-mean slope is 1; this fixes the unit of the scale |
| $\tau_\alpha$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln 0.5, 0.5)$ | spread of log slopes |
| $\sigma_b$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln \sigma_{\mathrm{median}}, \tau_\sigma)$ truncated to $(0, 0.5)$ | hierarchical noise SD per benchmark |
| $\sigma_{\mathrm{median}}$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln 0.1, 0.5)$ | typical noise SD |
| $\tau_\sigma$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln 0.5, 0.5)$ | spread of log $\sigma_b$ |

Only the three hyperpriors ($\tau_\alpha$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{median}}$, $\tau_\sigma$) can be changed through Epoch's interface. A joint draw from these priors is not a plausible dataset: about 89% of prior-simulated scores fall at the clip bounds and about 5% in $[0.1, 0.9]$ (calculated in `review_scratch/priorpred.py`). The flat priors are meant to be uninformative, not generative.

**The sampler.** `fit_bayesian_eci` runs NUTS (the standard adaptive Hamiltonian Monte Carlo sampler in PyMC) with 4 chains, target acceptance 0.95 and default seed 12345. It first runs a pilot with a diagonal mass matrix (1,000 tuning steps and 1,000 draws per chain), estimates the parameter covariance from it, and then samples with that dense mass matrix (500 tuning steps; 1,000 draws per chain, increased up to 16,000 until the checks pass). If that stage has any divergence or still fails at 16,000 draws, it falls back to a "cold" diagonal run (4,000 up to 32,000 draws). The convergence checks are: zero divergent transitions, $\mathrm{r\_hat} \le 1.01$ and bulk effective sample size (ESS) $\ge 400$ for capability, difficulty, slope, sigma and the three hyperparameters. Failures produce warnings, not errors. Epoch's README quotes about two minutes per fit.

**The anchors and the ECI scale.** The raw scale (c, d) has no meaningful unit, so every posterior draw $s$ is mapped linearly so that Claude 3.5 Sonnet sits at 130 and GPT-5 at 150:

```math
\mathrm{ECI}_m^{(s)} = 130 + 20 \times \frac{c_m^{(s)} - c_{\mathrm{low}}^{(s)}}{c_{\mathrm{high}}^{(s)} - c_{\mathrm{low}}^{(s)}},
```

with $\mathrm{low}$ = Claude 3.5 Sonnet and $\mathrm{high}$ = GPT-5. Difficulties are mapped the same way to give EDI, and slopes are divided by the per-draw scale factor. The published ECI is the mean of $\mathrm{ECI}_m^{(s)}$ over draws, and the published 90% credible interval is the 5th and 95th percentile. The two anchors have no interval because their values are fixed by definition. A consequence: any change that moves either anchor's raw capability relative to the other models moves every published score.

**What Epoch publishes** (folder `epoch_capabilities_index/` in the zip): `eci_scores.csv` (ECI and interval per model), `edi_scores.csv` (EDI and scaled slope per benchmark, without intervals or sigma), `eci_bootstraps.json` (500 posterior draws of every model's ECI and every benchmark's EDI and slope) and the fit input. The published 90% interval widths range from 2.9 to 41.8 ECI points (median 5.3); for the 20 highest-ranked models they are 4.2 to 7.6.

**Epoch's diagnostic suite** (`src/eci/evaluation.py`, run with `evaluate=True`): sampler diagnostics; accuracy of the fitted curve; PSIS-LOO (an importance-sampling approximation to leave-one-out cross-validation) with Pareto-k reliability bands; posterior predictive p-values, per benchmark and pooled, for nine statistics of the score distribution (mean, sd, variance, IQR, min, max, skew, share below 0.05, share above 0.95); and PIT calibration with coverage at 50, 80, 90 and 95%, in-sample and under PSIS-LOO. It has no prior predictive check, no prior sensitivity analysis, and no check of dimensionality, curve shape, time drift or developer effects.

**EEE.** Every Eval Ever (EEE) is an open dataset that stores published evaluation results from many sources in one JSON schema. The manifest used here (`ext/eee/entries.jsonl`, core hash dd7efcff, created 2026-11-13) lists 122,861 aggregate records in 129 collections. One record is one model evaluated by one source; it carries `source_metadata` (who produced the number), `model_info` (model id and name) and a list `evaluation_results`, each with a name, a `metric_config` (unit, minimum, maximum) and `score_details.score`.

**Same-upstream versus independent.** An EEE score and an Epoch score for the same model and benchmark are same-upstream when both were copied from one original measurement, for example the ARC Prize leaderboard, which Epoch cites and EEE also ingests. Agreement between them only shows that the crosswalk matched the right rows. They are independent when the EEE source ran the benchmark itself, separately from the measurement Epoch used. Only independent pairs test whether ECI predicts scores obtained elsewhere. The exact classification rule is in M9.

**Terms used below.**

- Posterior predictive check: simulate data from the fitted model and compare a statistic of the simulated data with the same statistic of the real data.
- PIT (probability integral transform): the share of the model's predictive draws at or below the observed value. A calibrated model gives uniform PIT values; a central 90% interval covers the observation exactly when $0.05 \le \mathrm{PIT} \le 0.95$.
- Coverage: the share of observations covered by their interval.
- Power-scaling sensitivity (psens): a diagnostic that reweights the existing posterior draws as if the prior had been raised to a power slightly different from 1 and measures how far a quantity's distribution moves (Kallioinen et al. 2024).
- Family-wise error rate: the probability of at least one false rejection among a set of tests.
- Simulation apparatus: the code that simulates data from a known model, refits it and scores the checks (blocks B1 and B3).

## Context

- Problem: Epoch's public code switched the ECI to a Bayesian model (merge commit e04929a, 2026-11-03) and Epoch now reports 90% credible intervals. Neither the ECI page nor the repository reports a prior sensitivity analysis or any posterior predictive check results. The repository ships a diagnostic suite behind `--evaluate` (described above) that has no prior predictive check, no prior sensitivity, and no checks aimed at the assumptions a one-dimensional index depends on.
- Evaluation goal: decide whether the published scores and intervals survive defensible prior changes (C1), whether the model fits the data in the ways that matter for the index (C2), and whether its intervals predict independently sourced scores held in EEE (C3).
- Operating mode: standalone.
- Upstream artifacts used: our Composite Index Model v1 spec (revision 3), which already describes ECI's model, and the v1 fit results (its cross-source coverage was 0.70; its local-dependence test failed). The v9 fit scripts and outputs were lost when the container was reset, so only numbers recorded in the conversation carry forward.
- Main constraints: 4 CPUs and 15 GB RAM. The fitting set is small (57 benchmarks, 256 models, 2,879 observations). Each fit uses all 4 CPUs (one chain per core), so fits run one after another; at Epoch's quoted two minutes per fit the plan needs about 13 hours of sampling, of which about 10 are the restartable simulations in B1. EEE parsing is I/O-bound.
- Dominant contribution: the first published prior sensitivity analysis and structural model checks for the Bayesian ECI, with checks whose error rate and power are measured by simulation.
- Critical reviewer concern: a clean result may come from perturbations that are too small, metrics that average over well-measured models, or checks that cannot reject. B1 exists to rule this out.
- Current evidence class: exploratory.
- Requested evidence class: confirmatory for C1 and C2 (pre-registered thresholds, self-reviewed, no independence claimed); exploratory for C3.
- Outcome-informed selection history: before writing this plan we read Epoch's code and README and checked the data zip's benchmark list, per-model observation counts and the published interval widths. We also looked at EEE collection names and 12 sampled records' source metadata. During the pre-run review the published 500-draw file `eci_bootstraps.json` was read to check that thresholds are attainable (details in `claim-map.json`, C1 selection history). No refit, residual or ECI-versus-EEE comparison was viewed.
- Material predecessor failures: on our own v1 model, full NUTS did not converge, cross-source coverage was 0.70, and the Q3 local-dependence test failed. Each is carried into the block that tests the same failure for ECI.

## Non-Vacuity Preflight

- Discriminating case: in the published results the models ranked 2 to 9 lie within 3.3 ECI points of each other, and the published draws give adjacent-pair ordering probabilities between 0.52 and 0.78 for seven of the nine adjacent pairs in the top 10. A prior that moves one of these models by 1.5 to 1.9 points (a quarter of its interval width) trips S2 and can reorder them, which S4 scores. Claude Fable 5.2 (19 scores, 8 mid-range) leads Gemini 4 Carbon (14 scores, 7 mid-range) by 3.68 points with 90% intervals 7.6 and 7.0 points wide; reversing that pair needs a relative shift of 3.7 points. The frontier stratum is scored on its own, so shifts there cannot be averaged away.
- Plausible comparator win: B1's positive-control prior (slopes forced nearly equal) must fail C1's rule; B1's planted misfits must be flagged by their targeted checks; in B4, same-upstream pairs must match and EEE-vs-EEE agreement sets the achievable bar.
- Complete loss or outcome contract: non-converged fits, unresolved grid entries, stress priors, non-converged simulation replicates, every crosswalk class and clipped scores are all reported with counts.
- Case-selection independence: the prior grid (`prior-grid.json`), check list, statistics, strata and thresholds were fixed before any refit. The frontier and sparse strata are defined from the published files, not from our fits.
- Skip, failure, null, and retry accounting: Epoch's own draw ladder runs inside every fit; a B2 fit that still fails gets one retry with draws doubled and is then recorded as unresolved, not dropped.
- Attainability check (added in review): every numeric gate was checked to be reachable and not automatic. Three were not and were changed (Revision Log R3, R6, R8).
- Gate result: pass.

## Methods Specification

The parts are numbered M1 to M10 and are cited from the JSON files. Unless stated, "draws" means all retained posterior draws pooled over chains, and "the fit input" means `processed_data_for_eci.csv`.

### M1. Quantities on the anchored scale

- ECI draw: $\mathrm{ECI}_m^{(s)}$ as defined in the orientation, computed per draw from the raw `capability` draws. Epoch's function returns these as `draws["eci"]` (shape draws x models).
- Point estimate $\mathrm{ECI}_m$: mean over draws. Interval: 5th and 95th percentile over draws (numpy `quantile`, default interpolation). Width $W_m$: 95th minus 5th percentile. $W_{m,0}$ is the width in the baseline fit.
- Shift of model $m$ under prior $k$: $\Delta_{m,k} = \mathrm{ECI}_{m,k} - \mathrm{ECI}_{m,0}$. Relative shift: $\lvert\Delta_{m,k}\rvert / W_{m,0}$.
- Width ratio: $W_{m,k} / W_{m,0}$.
- $P(i > j)$: the share of draws $s$ with $\mathrm{ECI}_i^{(s)} > \mathrm{ECI}_j^{(s)}$, using the same draw index for both models.
- Anchors: Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-5 have ECI fixed at 130 and 150 in every draw. They are included in rank-based metrics (S1, S4) and excluded from shift, width and psens metrics. There are 254 non-anchor models.
- Raw-scale versions of the shift and width use `capability` draws directly; they are reported as secondary and do not enter any rule.

### M2. Strata

Fixed from the published files before any fit and stored in `runs/strata.json`:

- all: the 256 models (254 non-anchor).
- frontier: the 20 models with the highest `eci` in the published `eci_scores.csv` (Claude Fable 5.2 down to GPT-5.5; published ECI 173.44 to 160.10). None is an anchor and none is sparse.
- sparse: the 72 models with 6 or fewer rows in the fit input.
- dense: the 184 models with 7 or more rows (182 non-anchor). Reported; used only to state the narrowed form of C1.

### M3. Sensitivity metrics S1 to S5

Baseline for B2 is fit P0 (default priors, B2 sampler settings). For each alternative prior $k$ and each stratum:

- S1: Spearman rank correlation between the vectors $\mathrm{ECI}_{m,k}$ and $\mathrm{ECI}_{m,0}$ over the stratum's models (anchors included). Pass if $\ge 0.99$.
- S2: share of the stratum's non-anchor models with relative shift $> 0.25$. Pass if $\le 5\%$, that is at most 12 of 254 (all), 1 of 20 (frontier), 3 of 72 (sparse), 9 of 182 (dense).
- S3: share of the stratum's non-anchor models with width ratio outside $[0.8, 1.25]$. Pass if $\le 5\%$ (same counts).
- S4: (a) the set of the 10 models with the highest posterior-mean ECI is the same under $k$ as under P0; (b) for each of the 9 adjacent pairs (rank $r$, rank $r+1$), $r = 1..9$, in the P0 order, $\lvert P_k(i > j) - P_0(i > j)\rvert < 0.10$. S4 is scored once and counts in the frontier and all-models strata.
- S5 (screen, no refit): on P0's draws, compute the log density of the three hyperpriors at each draw (sum of three LogNormal log-pdfs; the flat capability and difficulty priors are constants, and the hierarchical priors of sigma_b and log a_b are deliberately left out, following the usual advice to power-scale top-level priors only). Compute psens with the ArviZ implementation (`psens`, group "prior", default step $\alpha = 0.99$ and 1.01) for each non-anchor model's ECI draws, jointly for the three hyperpriors and separately for each. Pass in a stratum if psens $\le 0.05$ for at least 95% of its non-anchor models. Also reported, not gated: psens for $\tau_\alpha$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{median}}$ and $\tau_\sigma$ themselves, and importance-reweighted ECI means at $\alpha = 0.8$ and 1.25 with the Pareto-k of the weights.
- S5 follow-up: raising $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\mu, s)$ to the power $a$ gives $\operatorname{LogNormal}\bigl(\mu + s^2 (1 - a) / a,\ s / \sqrt{a}\bigr)$. If S5 flags a hyperprior, the two exact refits at $a = 0.8$ and $a = 1.25$ for that hyperprior are run and scored with S1-S4 like grid entries; they decide in place of the screen. These perturbations are smaller than P1-P7, so S5 is a consistency check on the refits, not an independent stress.
- Noise floor: every metric is also computed for P0b (default priors, second seed) against P0. The floor must show max relative shift $\le 0.05$ and max adjacent-pair probability change $\le 0.03$; otherwise draws are doubled for all B2 fits, once.
- Reported beside S4: P(model in top 10) for each model, under each prior. In the published draws the 10th and 11th models are 0.55 points apart and the 10th has probability 0.62 of being in the top 10, so S4(a) can fail on a small shift (see Open Judgement Calls).

### M4. Simulation generators (B1)

Common to all generators. The cell list is the 2,879 (model, benchmark) rows of the fit input, with their `model_id`, `benchmark_id`, `Model`, `benchmark`, `date` and `benchmark_release_date` columns unchanged. A simulated dataset replaces only `performance`. Epoch's inclusion rule is not re-applied to simulated data. Replicate $r$ of generator $g$ uses seed = the first 4 bytes of sha256("g:r") read as a big-endian integer, for both simulation and fitting.

- G-null. Take one joint draw $\theta_r = (c, d, a, \sigma)$ from the B0 seed-12345 posterior, at position $\lfloor r \times S / 199 \rfloor$ of the pooled draws ($S$ = number of pooled draws). Compute $\mu$ and $\kappa$ as in the model and draw $y \sim \operatorname{Beta}(\mu \kappa,\ (1 - \mu) \kappa)$. The fit applies its usual clip. 199 replicates.
- G-2D (second dimension). As G-null, then give each model a second capability $c2_m = \bar{c} + 0.7\,(c_m - \bar{c}) + \sqrt{1 - 0.49} \times s_c \times e_m$, with $e_m$ standard normal and $\bar{c}$, $s_c$ the mean and SD of $c$ over models in this draw, so that $c$ and $c2$ have correlation 0.7 and equal spread. A random 28 of the 57 benchmarks use $c2$ in place of $c$. 20 replicates.
- G-offset (developer effect). As G-null, then pick one developer at random from the seven organisations with at least 100 rows (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Alibaba, Meta AI, DeepSeek, xAI; the `Organization` column of `eci_scores.csv`) and 3 benchmarks at random from those where that developer has at least 5 rows; add 0.5 to the logit $a_b\,(c_m - d_b)$ in those cells. 20 replicates.
- G-shape (curve shape). As G-null, then for a random 19 of the 57 benchmarks multiply $\mu$ by 0.85 (an upper asymptote below the stated ceiling). 20 replicates.
- G-disp (dispersion form). As G-null for $\mu$, but $y = \mu + \sigma_b \times e$ with $e$ standard normal, clipped to $[0, 1]$, so the noise SD does not shrink toward 0 and 1. 20 replicates.
- G-drift (time drift). As G-null, then add $0.3 \times \min(3, \max(0, g_{mb}))$ to the logit, where $g_{mb}$ is the number of years from the benchmark's release date to the model's date. 20 replicates.
- G-prior (prior predictive; no fits). Draw 35 complete parameter sets from the default priors (difficulties drawn as uniform on $[-50, 50]$ and centred to sum to 0), simulate the 2,879 cells for each, and report the share of scores at or beyond the clip bounds and the share in $[0.1, 0.9]$. This is descriptive: the flat priors make the first share about 0.89 by construction.
- Positive control (real data, not simulated). Refit the real data with $\tau_\alpha \sim \operatorname{LogNormal}(\ln 0.05, 0.1)$, which forces nearly equal slopes, for seeds 12345, 22345 and 32345, and score S1-S4 against the B0 fit of the same seed.

Each simulation fit calls `fit_bayesian_eci(df_r, random_seed=seed, evaluate=False, progressbar=False)` with default priors and Epoch's default adaptive draws. A replicate is converged if Epoch's three checks pass on the returned trace. Recovery (G-null only): the true anchored value $\mathrm{ECI}^*_m = 130 + 20\,(c_m - c_{\mathrm{low}}) / (c_{\mathrm{high}} - c_{\mathrm{low}})$ from $\theta_r$ is covered if it lies within the replicate fit's [5th, 95th] percentile interval. Recovery coverage is the share covered over all converged replicates and non-anchor models, overall and per stratum, with a 90% interval from resampling replicates.

Effect sizes: the 0.7 correlation and the 0.5 logit offset are from the original plan. G-shape, G-disp and G-drift were added in review so that T1, T3 and T4 have a planted misfit; their sizes are the reviewer's choice (see Open Judgement Calls).

### M5. Check statistics T1 to T7

Each statistic is a function of a dataset and of the fit to that same dataset. Use 400 evenly spaced posterior draws. For row $i = (m, b)$, with $y_i$ the performance clipped to $[0.001, 0.999]$:

- $\mu_{is}$ and $\kappa_{bs}$ as in the model, per draw $s$; $\bar{\mu}_i$ = mean over $s$ of $\mu_{is}$;
- $v_i$ = mean over $s$ of $\mu_{is} (1 - \mu_{is}) / (\kappa_{bs} + 1)$, plus the variance over $s$ of $\mu_{is}$ (the predictive variance);
- standardised residual $z_i = (y_i - \bar{\mu}_i) / \sqrt{v_i}$;
- $\hat{c}_m$ = posterior mean of the raw capability.

Eligibility sets are fixed from the real observation pattern and reused for every simulated dataset.

- T1 (item-curve shape). For each of the 44 benchmarks with at least 20 rows: sort its rows by $\hat{c}_m$, split into 4 consecutive groups of near-equal size (`numpy.array_split`), and compute $Q_b$ = sum over groups of $n_g \times (\text{mean of } z \text{ in the group})^2$. T1 = mean of $Q_b$ over the 44 benchmarks.
- T2 (local dependence, a Q3-type statistic). For each of the 445 benchmark pairs that share at least 15 models: $r$ = Pearson correlation of $z$ over the shared models, $f = \operatorname{atanh}(r)$ with $r$ clipped to $[-0.999, 0.999]$, $q = (n - 3) f^2$. T2 = mean of $q$ over the 445 pairs. Reported beside it: max $\lvert r\rvert$ and the share of pairs with $\lvert r\rvert > 0.3$.
- T3 (dispersion form). Bin rows by $\bar{\mu}$ into $[0, 0.1)$, $[0.1, 0.3)$, $[0.3, 0.7)$, $[0.7, 0.9)$, $[0.9, 1]$; a bin with fewer than 30 rows is merged with its neighbour toward 0.5. $D_g$ = mean of $z^2$ in bin $g$. T3 = sum over bins of $(n_g / N) \times (\ln D_g)^2$.
- T4 (time drift). $\mathrm{gap}_i$ = days from `benchmark_release_date` to the model's `date` (negative allowed). T4 = $\lvert\text{Spearman correlation of } z_i \text{ and } \mathrm{gap}_i\rvert$ over all rows. Reported beside it: the Spearman correlation of $z$ with model date within each of the 44 benchmarks. (Within one benchmark the gap and the model date give the same ordering, so the original plan's two versions of this check coincide there.)
- T5 (developer x benchmark). Developer = `Organization` in `eci_scores.csv`, used verbatim; models with no organisation are left out. Among the 13 organisations with at least 20 rows, take every (organisation, benchmark) cell with at least 5 rows (185 cells). $t = (\text{sum of } z \text{ in the cell}) / \sqrt{n}$. T5 = max $\lvert t\rvert$ over cells. A developer's average residual over all benchmarks is not used, because each model's own capability absorbs it.
- T6 (boundary mass). $N_0$ = number of rows with $y \le 0.001$, $N_1$ = number with $y \ge 0.999$. $E_0$ = sum over rows of the mean over $s$ of the Beta CDF at 0.001; $E_1$ = sum over rows of the mean over $s$ of one minus the Beta CDF at 0.999. T6 = $(N_0 - E_0)^2 / (E_0 + 1) + (N_1 - E_1)^2 / (E_1 + 1)$. The per-benchmark version $c_b$ uses that benchmark's rows only.
- T7 (per-model fit). For each of the 217 models with at least 6 rows: $U_m$ = mean of $z^2$ over its rows. T7 = max of $U_m$.

All seven are non-negative and larger means worse fit. Targeted generators: T1 G-shape, T2 G-2D, T3 G-disp, T4 G-drift, T5 G-offset. T6 and T7 have none; a pass on them is labelled "power not assessed".

### M6. Calibrated p-values, family-wise adjustment, power and localisation

Reference set: the $T$ values of the $R'$ converged G-null replicates (target 199, minimum 150). Under G-null the data come from the fitted model family with parameters drawn from the B0 posterior, and each replicate is refitted, so the reference distribution includes the effect of estimating parameters from the same data. This replaces the conservative in-sample posterior predictive p-value.

- Unadjusted $p_k = (1 + \text{number of null replicates with } T_{k,r} \ge T_{k,\mathrm{obs}}) / (R' + 1)$.
- Family-wise adjustment (single-step max-T). Let $l_k = \ln(T_k + 1\mathrm{e}{-12})$. For each check $k$ compute the mean and SD of $l_k$ over the $R' + 1$ values (observed plus null) and standardise: $z_{k,j} = (l_{k,j} - \mathrm{mean}_k) / \mathrm{SD}_k$. For each null replicate $r$ let $M_r$ = max over $k$ of $z_{k,r}$. Adjusted $p_k = (1 + \text{number of } r \text{ with } M_r \ge z_{k,\mathrm{obs}}) / (R' + 1)$.
- Rejection: adjusted $p_k < 0.01$. With $R' = 199$ this happens only when $z_{k,\mathrm{obs}}$ exceeds $M_r$ for every null replicate (adjusted $p = 0.005$). With $R'$ between 150 and 198 the same holds. This controls the family-wise error rate at or below 0.01 under G-null.
- Raw in-sample $p$ (reported only): for each of the 400 draws, simulate $y_{\mathrm{rep}}$ from that draw, recompute $T_k$ on $y_{\mathrm{rep}}$ with the residual quantities ($\bar{\mu}$, $v$, $\hat{c}$) held at their real-data values, and report the share of draws with $T_k(y_{\mathrm{rep}}) \ge T_k(y)$.
- Power: a misfit replicate is evaluated exactly like the observed data (its own refit, the same reference set). Power of check $k$ against its targeted generator = share of converged replicates with adjusted $p_k < 0.01$. The requirement "power $\ge 0.8$" means at least 16 of 20, and at least 15 replicates must have converged. The 90% Clopper-Pearson interval is reported.
- Localisation (used only after a rejection or a coverage flag). Components: $Q_b$ for T1, $q$ for each pair for T2, $\lvert t\rvert$ for each cell for T5, $c_b$ for T6. A component is extreme if it exceeds the 99th percentile, over null replicates, of the largest component of that type in the replicate. $F$ is the union of: benchmarks with an extreme $Q_b$ or $c_b$; both benchmarks of an extreme pair; the benchmark of an extreme cell; and benchmarks flagged by the held-out coverage test (M7). A rejected check $k$ is localised to $F$ if, after deleting the rows of benchmarks in $F$ from the residual table (no refit) for the observed data and for every null replicate, the unadjusted $p_k$ is at least 0.01. T3, T4 and T7 have no components; they are localised only if this deletion test passes with the $F$ obtained from the other checks.
- B3d. If $F$ is non-empty and has at most 10 benchmarks: delete those rows from the fit input, re-apply Epoch's inclusion rule (at least 4 benchmarks and at least 4 mid-range per model), refit with default settings, and compute the relative shift (M1) for every model present in both fits. Models dropped by the inclusion rule are listed. If an anchor is dropped, B3d is not computable and the verdict is Inadequate.

### M7. Held-out folds and coverage (B3c)

- Eligible models: the 217 with at least 6 rows (2,704 rows). The 39 models with 4 or 5 rows (175 rows) stay in every training set and are never predicted.
- Assignment: `rng = numpy.random.default_rng(20261113)`; for each eligible model in the order of first appearance in the fit input, permute its rows with `rng.permutation` and give the row at permuted position $p$ the fold $p \bmod 5$. Every eligible row is held out exactly once, and each fold removes between $\lfloor n/5 \rfloor$ and $\lceil n/5 \rceil$ of a model's $n$ rows (about 20%). If any benchmark would have fewer than 3 training rows in some fold, redraw with seed 20261114. The assignment is written to `runs/B3/folds.csv` before any fold is fitted.
- Fit: for fold $f$, fit the rows not in fold $f$ with default settings and seed $12345 + f$. The inclusion rule is not re-applied.
- Prediction (M8) for each held-out row, PIT against the clipped observed score, covered if $0.05 \le \mathrm{PIT} \le 0.95$.
- Overall coverage = covered rows / 2,704, with a 90% interval from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of models. Rule: in $[0.85, 0.95]$.
- Per benchmark: for each of the 44 benchmarks with at least 20 held-out rows, an exact two-sided binomial test (`scipy.stats.binomtest`) of the covered count against 0.90. Flag if $p < 0.01 / 44$.
- Reported for comparison: Epoch's PSIS-LOO coverage (`coverage_0.9_loo`) from B3a.

### M8. How ECI predicts one score

To predict the score of model $m$ on benchmark $b$ from a fit that contains both $m$ and $b$: for each posterior draw $s$ compute $\mu_s = \operatorname{sigmoid}\bigl(a_b^{(s)} (c_m^{(s)} - d_b^{(s)})\bigr)$ clipped to $[1\mathrm{e}{-6},\ 1 - 1\mathrm{e}{-6}]$ and $\kappa_b^{(s)}$, and draw $y_{\mathrm{rep}}^{(s)} \sim \operatorname{Beta}(\mu_s \kappa,\ (1 - \mu_s) \kappa)$. Use 2,000 evenly spaced draws. The predictive mean is the mean of $\mu_s$; the 90% predictive interval is the 5th and 95th percentile of $y_{\mathrm{rep}}$.

- Target score: a value on Epoch's normalised scale, $(\text{raw as a proportion} - \mathrm{random\_baseline}_b) / (\mathrm{score\_ceiling}_b - \mathrm{random\_baseline}_b)$, clipped to $[0, 1]$ and then to $[0.001, 0.999]$. The baseline and ceiling come from `benchmark_metadata.csv`.
- PIT = share of draws with $y_{\mathrm{rep}}^{(s)} \le \text{target}$. This is Epoch's own definition (`evaluation._pit`), so results are comparable with its coverage table.
- If Epoch's own score for (m, b) is in the fit, weight the draws by that row's normalised PSIS-LOO weights (from `az.loo(trace, pointwise=True)`, as in `evaluation.loo_diagnostics`), so the prediction does not use Epoch's score for the same pair. Report the row's Pareto $k$; $k > 0.7$ marks the weights as unreliable.
- A model or benchmark that is not in the fit cannot be predicted and is excluded with a reason.

### M9. EEE crosswalk, source classes and audit (B4)

Stage a is completed and frozen before any ECI prediction is loaded.

1. Download. Fetch every `object_path` in `entries.jsonl` from the EEE datastore into `runs/B4/objects/` and verify each file's sha256 against the manifest. Failed downloads are listed.
2. Flatten. One row per `evaluation_results` element: collection, object id, `model_info.id`, `model_info.name`, `source_metadata.source_name`, `source_organization_name`, `source_type`, `evaluator_relationship`, `evaluation_name`, `source_data.dataset_name`, `metric_config` (metric id, unit, min_score, max_score, lower_is_better), `score_details.score`.
3. Benchmark map (`runs/B4/benchmark_map.csv`, by hand). A row is a candidate if the normalised text (lowercase, non-alphanumerics removed) of its collection, evaluation name or dataset name contains the normalised name or a listed alias of one of the 57 ECI benchmarks. For each distinct (collection, evaluation name, dataset name, metric id) among candidates, record the ECI benchmark, and whether the version (for example FrontierMath tier and date, Terminal-Bench 2.0, ARC-AGI 1 or 2), the split (for example semi-private or public) and the metric (the same quantity as Epoch's `score_column`) match. Include only when all three match; otherwise class version-mismatch or metric-mismatch. Record the scale that converts the EEE score to a proportion (1 if max_score is 1, 0.01 if it is 100). All 129 collections appear in `collection_screen.csv` with the number of candidate rows.
4. Model map (`runs/B4/model_map.csv`). Normalise `model_info.id` and `name` (lowercase; drop the developer prefix before "/"; replace spaces, underscores and dots with hyphens). Match against the normalised `model_version` values of `model_metadata.csv`, then against `display_name` and `model_group`. Exactly one group: mapped. More than one group, or a match that depends on a date or reasoning-setting suffix that the EEE id lacks: ambiguous, reviewed by hand and either resolved with a written reason or left as ambiguous-model. No match: model-unmapped. A group that is not among the 256 fitted models: model-not-in-fit.
5. Source class per pair (`runs/B4/source_class.csv` holds the per-source part). Compare the EEE record's source with the `source` column of Epoch's row for the same model group and benchmark:
   - same-upstream: the EEE source organisation is the organisation or leaderboard Epoch's row cites; or the EEE record relays a developer-reported score (`evaluator_relationship` is first_party, including llm-stats first-party records) and Epoch's row cites the developer's own paper or report; or the EEE source is Epoch.
   - independent: the EEE source ran the evaluation itself (`source_type` is evaluation_run, or a third-party leaderboard that runs its own evaluations) and is not the source Epoch's row cites. If Epoch's benchmark file has no `_external` suffix (Epoch ran it), any non-Epoch EEE source is independent.
   - upstream-unknown: anything else, including aggregators of unstated provenance and Epoch rows on external benchmarks with an empty `source`. Reported separately; not part of the C3 coverage.
6. Pairs. A pair is (model group, ECI benchmark, EEE source organisation). Its score is the highest normalised score among that source's records for the pair, mirroring Epoch's best-across-versions rule; the other records are class duplicate.
7. Audit. Draw 50 pairs at random (`default_rng(20261113)`) from matched-independent and matched-same-upstream pairs. For each, open the EEE JSON and Epoch's rows and check four things: (i) the EEE model is a version that `model_metadata.csv` lists under the mapped group; (ii) benchmark, version and split are the ones Epoch uses; (iii) metric and scale are right and the normalised score recomputed by hand equals the stored one; (iv) the source class follows rule 5. A pair with any failed item is one error. Results go to `runs/B4/audit_sample.csv` with one column per item. Pass: at most 2 errors in 50. The Clopper-Pearson 95% upper bound on the error rate is reported (5.8% for 0 errors, 12.1% for 2). On failure: fix the cause, rebuild, and audit a fresh sample of 50 drawn with seed 20261114; a second failure stops B4. The audit is done by the analyst who built the maps, so it is a self-check.
8. Freeze. Record the sha256 of the three map files in `runs/freeze.json`. Stage b refuses to run if they differ.

Stage b:

- Same-upstream control: for matched-same-upstream pairs, $\lvert\text{EEE normalised score} - \text{Epoch's normalised score in the fit input}\rvert$. Requirement: median $\le 0.02$.
- Coverage: PIT (M8) for every matched-independent pair; coverage = share with $0.05 \le \mathrm{PIT} \le 0.95$; 90% interval from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of model groups. Reported also by benchmark, by whether Epoch's own score for the pair is in the fit, and with two sensitivity cuts (pairs with Pareto $k > 0.7$ removed; pairs whose raw score equals Epoch's to 3 decimals removed).
- Verdict label: `harness-limited` is the registered name of the C3 verdict given when independent sources disagree with each other more than the model's noise allows (agreement below 0.80). It means the comparison is limited by differences in evaluation setup between sources, not by ECI.
- EEE-vs-EEE agreement: for every (model group, benchmark) with at least 2 independent sources, each unordered pair of sources agrees if the absolute difference of their normalised scores is at most $\sqrt{2} \times (q_{95} - q_{05}) / 2$, where $q_{05}$ and $q_{95}$ are the unweighted ECI predictive quantiles for that model and benchmark. If the two scores were independent draws from the ECI predictive distribution, about 90% would agree. Agreement is estimable with at least 30 source pairs.
- Bias: mean of $(\text{EEE normalised score} - \text{ECI predictive mean})$ per benchmark. Epoch's best-of rule predicts a negative sign for sources that report a single run.

### M10. Engineering conventions

- Working directory for every command: the `v11/` root. External inputs are under `ext/` and are never written to; no interpreter is started with its working directory inside `ext/`.
- Environment: `runs/` is a uv project (Python >= 3.12). Epoch's code is installed from `runs/vendor/eci-public`, a copy of `ext/eci-public` verified identical file by file (excluding `.git`); the commit hash and the comparison result are recorded in `runs/freeze.json`. Pin pymc (>= 6.0), pytensor, arviz (>= 1.0), numpy, scipy and pandas to the versions in Epoch's `uv.lock`; add pyarrow. Write `uv pip freeze` to `runs/env.lock.txt`. Installing packages and downloading EEE objects need network access; nothing else does.
- Layout: entrypoints `runs/b0_reproduce.py`, `b1_simulate.py`, `b2_sensitivity.py`, `b3_ppc.py`, `b4_run.py`, `b5_eee_refit.py`, `b6_handling.py`; shared code in `runs/lib/` (`data.py` reads the zip, `fit.py` wraps `fit_bayesian_eci` and the sidecar logic, `anchored.py` M1, `sens_metrics.py` M3, `variants.py` P8-P10, `generators.py` M4, `ppc_checks.py` M5-M6, `predict.py` M8); outputs in `runs/B0/` to `runs/B6/`.
- Idempotency: every fit writes `<name>.nc` and `<name>.json` to a temporary name and renames both when complete. The JSON sidecar records the sha256 of the fit input, the sha256 of the code files it depends on, the prior, seed, sampler arguments, Epoch's `attempts` list, `started_from`, the convergence verdict and all warnings. On restart a fit is skipped only if its sidecar exists and its recorded digests and arguments equal the current ones. Summary files (`reproduction.json`, `calibration.json`, `sensitivity.json`, `ppc.json`, `transfer.json`) are rebuilt from the sidecars on every run.
- Storage: B0 traces are saved in full (about 0.2 GB each with the log-likelihood and predictive groups). B1 replicates save 400 thinned draws and their T values only. B2 saves the posterior group only.
- Frozen files: `experiment-plan/prior-grid.json`, `runs/lib/sens_metrics.py`, `runs/lib/ppc_checks.py`, `runs/strata.json` and, for B4, the three maps. Their sha256 digests are written to `runs/freeze.json` at the point each block's rules say, and every entrypoint checks them before running.
- Version note: arviz >= 1.0 returns the trace as an xarray DataTree; `to_netcdf` and `open_datatree` round-trip it. Confirm the `psens` signature against the pinned version when writing `sens_metrics.py`.

## Risks and Confounds

- Risk: the published file comes from Epoch's production pipeline, which may differ from the public defaults. The public loader already selects different model versions for three GPQA diamond rows. Mitigation: B0 fits the published fit input, reports the loader-input fit as B0b, and G0 decides whether to rebaseline.
- Risk: Monte Carlo error is mistaken for prior sensitivity or for a reproduction failure. Mitigation: 4,000 draws per chain in B2, a second-seed noise floor for every metric, and a G0 tolerance scaled to interval width.
- Risk: flat priors could let sparse models drift toward the $\pm 50$ bounds. The published draws stay within abs 12.6 on the raw scale, so this is not expected; B2 still reports posterior mass beyond abs 49.
- Risk: the anchor transform (Claude 3.5 Sonnet = 130, GPT-5 = 150) moves every score when a prior moves either anchor. Mitigation: S1 and S4 are scale-free; S2 and S3 are also reported on the raw scale; B6 swaps the anchors.
- Risk: in-sample posterior predictive p-values are conservative. Mitigation: p-values come from refitted null simulations (M6).
- Risk: the null simulations use parameters drawn from the posterior of the same data the checks are applied to. This is a parametric bootstrap and is valid for testing the model family, but the reference distribution is conditional on the fitted parameter region; recovery and power results hold for that region only.
- Risk: checks without a planted misfit (T6, T7) or with low measured power pass uninformatively. Mitigation: they are labelled and cannot support "Adequate".
- Risk: EEE and Epoch share upstreams, so a match there is transcription, not replication. Mitigation: per-pair source class (M9); same-upstream pairs are used only as the positive control.
- Risk: Epoch keeps the best score across a model group's versions, so single-run independent scores are expected to be lower. Mitigation: signed bias per benchmark is reported and read together with coverage.
- Risk: model-name crosswalk errors. Mitigation: a four-item audit of 50 pairs with at most 2 errors, and every ambiguous case excluded with a reason.
- Risk: benchmark version drift (FrontierMath tiers and dates, Terminal-Bench versions, ARC-AGI splits). Mitigation: versions are mapped by hand and mismatches excluded before scoring.
- Risk: Epoch's data change daily. Mitigation: the 2026-11-13 zip is pinned by sha256, and every result names it.

## Open Judgement Calls

These were left for the plan owner. The plan runs as written if none is changed.

1. Baseline data. The plan now treats the published fit input as the baseline and the loader output as a reported variant (B0b). The opposite choice is defensible if the aim is "what the public code produces today". The three differing rows include the top-ranked model.
2. S4(a), "top-10 set unchanged". In the published draws the 10th model has probability 0.62 of being in the top 10 and leads the 11th by 0.55 points, so a shift well inside the S2 tolerance can fail S4(a) and falsify C1. The rule is kept as registered; $P(\text{in top 10})$ is reported so a reader can judge. A probability-based version (no model's $P(\text{in top 10})$ changes by 0.10 or more) would be more stable.
3. P8, $\operatorname{Normal}(0, 3)$ on raw capability. The published raw capability means run from -8.9 to 3.2, so the weakest models sit about 3 prior SDs from the prior centre and P8 is informative for them. It is kept as a defensible proper alternative; if the owner sees it as a stress prior it should move to the X list before any fit. The guard that rescales P8 when the spread exceeds 3 is discontinuous at 3.
4. Sizes of the added misfit generators (0.85 asymptote on a third of benchmarks; constant-SD noise; 0.3 logit per year). They set what "power" means for T1, T3 and T4.
5. The family-wise test rejects only when the observed statistic exceeds all 199 null replicates. More null replicates (999) would give finer p-values at about five times the B1 cost.
6. C3 verdicts use point estimates. With few independent pairs the bootstrap interval may span two verdict bands; the minimum of 100 pairs is the reviewer's choice.
7. The audit rule (at most 2 errors in 50) leaves a 95% upper bound of 12% on the true error rate. A sample of 100 with at most 2 errors would bound it at 6.2%.
8. EEE datastore location (resolved 2026-11-13 by the plan owner): objects come from the Hugging Face dataset `evaleval/EEE_datastore` at each `object_path` in manifest dd7efcff; every download is verified against its manifest sha256, so the pin is by content.
