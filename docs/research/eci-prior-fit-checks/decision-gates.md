Definitions of every quantity are in the "Methods Specification" section of experiment-plan.md (M1 to M10). $W$ is a model's 90% interval width; for G0 it is the published width, elsewhere the width in the baseline fit. The decision rules in claim-map.json are the authority; this table restates them as gate branches.

## Non-vacuity preflight (recorded before execution)

- **Discriminating case.** In the published results the models ranked 2 to 9 lie within 3.3 ECI points, and seven of the nine adjacent pairs in the top 10 have ordering probabilities between 0.52 and 0.78, so slope and noise priors can reorder them. 72 of 256 models have 6 or fewer scores. Both strata are scored separately, so a shift there cannot be averaged away.
- **Comparator can win.** For C1, a deliberately strong slope prior (B1 positive control) must fail the rule; for C2, planted misfit must be detected by the targeted check; for C3, same-upstream pairs must match and the EEE-vs-EEE agreement rate sets the bar a perfect index could reach.
- **Thresholds are attainable and not automatic.** Checked in review: the G0 tolerance against Monte Carlo error in the published draws; the smallest attainable adjusted p-value (0.005) against the 0.01 level; the per-benchmark coverage rule against binomial variation. Three original rules failed this check and were replaced (Revision Log R3, R6, R8 in experiment-plan.md).
- **Complete outcome contract.** Non-converged fits and replicates, unresolved grid entries, every crosswalk class and clipped observations all stay in the accounting with counts (see each block's `complete_outcome_accounting`).
- **Case-selection independence.** Prior grid, check statistics, strata and thresholds were written from Epoch's documented priors, the observation pattern and the published summary files; no refit, residual or EEE-vs-ECI comparison had been viewed.
- **Gate result: pass.** C1 and C2 can be promoted to confirmatory (pre-registered, self-reviewed, no independence claimed). C3 stays exploratory because crosswalk choices need judgement.
