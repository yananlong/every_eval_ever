## Canonical Facts

### Problem and estimand

- Question: for a new model, can any subset of its known benchmark scores predict all remaining scores, with neither the input benchmarks nor the targets fixed in advance (body:91-92).
- Estimand: the publicly reported aggregate score s_mb of model m on benchmark b, on the benchmark's native scale (mostly 0 to 100) (body:178, body:466). The authors state the target is "what this public matrix would extrapolate to rather than what a controlled re-evaluation under a single configuration would yield" (body:120-121).
- Stated scope: the analysis identifies scores that are currently inferable, "not benchmarks whose existence is unnecessary" (body:124-125).

### Data

- Collection: seed a queue with recent frontier models, crawl official sources (release blog, system card, technical report, HF model card), recurse on every competitor mentioned, then sweep six primary leaderboards (MathArena, ARC-Prize, Terminal-Bench, LMArena, Epoch AI, LiveBench) (body:185-189).
- Conflict rule: highest-priority source wins (release blog > system card > technical report > HF model card > primary leaderboards), ties by recency. One canonical configuration per model, "typically the setting the vendor itself foregrounds" (body:191-192).
- Provenance: every value carries a URL (body:193). "Roughly half come from a source outside the model's own provider" (body:193) but Figure 3e caption says "Roughly four in five scores come from the model provider's own materials" (body:335). See open questions.
- Raw audit pool (May 2026): 188 models x 316 benchmarks, 4,493 of 59,408 cells (7.6%) (body:223, body:262). Canonicalised pool: 181 models, 304 benchmarks, 4,177 cells (body:267).
- Density filter: at least 15 observed benchmarks per model and 8 observed models per benchmark, iterated to a fixed point, gives **84 x 133, 2,604 of 11,172 cells, 23.3% fill** (Table 1, body:225-234; body:273, body:285). The authors say "other choices are equally reasonable" (body:271).
- Models: 13 providers (OpenAI 20, Google 12, Anthropic 11, Alibaba/Qwen 11, DeepSeek 9, Meta 6, Zhipu 4, Moonshot 3, xAI 3, MiniMax 2, Cohere 1, ByteDance 1, Mistral 1); 51 reasoning, 31 non-reasoning; 35 open-weight, 47 closed; parameters disclosed for 25 (body:294-297, body:1028).
- Benchmarks by category: agentic and tool use 26, math 23, coding 21, multimodal 12, long context 9, instruction following 9, knowledge and QA 9, reasoning 8, other 7, hallucination 5, science 4 (app:223-365). Many have tiny item counts (AIME 30, CNMO 6, USAMO 6, MathArena Apex 12) (app:250-272).
- Non-percentage columns: Codeforces rating, Chatbot Arena Elo, GDPval ELO (body:478). Lower-is-better columns exist (OmniDocBench edit distance, hallucination rates) (app:302-303, app:349-351); no sign flip is described.
- Granularity: benchmark-level aggregate scores only. No item-level data (body:158, body:1165).
- Missingness: acknowledged as structured; "Popular models x popular benchmarks are over-represented, violating the uniform-sampling assumption underlying standard matrix-completion guarantees" (body:250-252). Coverage varies with release date (body:335). No missingness model is fit.
- Heterogeneity acknowledged: prompting, reasoning budget, temperature, harness, version, date; vendor-reported scores "may be optimistically biased"; decoding noise 1 to 3 points, harness shifts above 5 points (body:245-248).
- Version 2 (2026-08-26): 283 models, 712 benchmarks, 8,713 primary scores; filtered 129 x 253 with 4,905 cells (15.0%) (app:72-75). The HF dataset card's "paper matrix" is this August matrix, not the May 84 x 133 matrix used for the paper's experiments (card "Matrix construction").

### Transform and preprocessing

- Seven transforms tested: identity, log(s+1), logit log(s / (100 - s)), arcsinh(s/50), sqrt, probit, within-benchmark quantile (body:468-477).
- Range-assuming transforms apply only to percentage-scale benchmarks; the three non-percentage benchmarks are left untransformed. Logit and probit inputs are clipped "slightly away from the endpoints" (clip value not given) (body:478-479).
- After the transform each benchmark column is standardised to zero mean and unit variance using observed training entries; predictions are un-standardised, inverse-transformed and clipped to [0, 100] (body:480-482, body:655).

### Model (BenchPress default)

- Bias-decomposed ALS (Koren et al. 2009) in standardised logit space. Prediction x-hat_mb = x-bar + (x-bar_m. - x-bar) + (x-bar_.b - x-bar) + (U V^T)_mb, with U in R^{M x R}, V in R^{B x R} (body:527-552, app:612-635).
- Objective: sum over observed cells of squared residual after the additive terms, plus lambda (||U||_F^2 + ||V||_F^2) (body:531-543).
- The offsets are **observed means**, not jointly estimated parameters (body:530, app:530-532). [shown]
- Defaults: R = 2, lambda = 0.1, average of completed matrices from 10 random initialisations (seeds 42 to 51) (app:636; body:603).
- No stated probabilistic model, no estimated noise variance and no identifiability constraint on (U, V). The objective is the penalised Gaussian log-likelihood of an implicit model: squared error in standardised logit space is the negative log-likelihood of independent Gaussian noise with a common variance, and the ridge term is a Gaussian prior on the rows of U and V with the ratio of noise to prior variance fixed at the penalty weight, so ALS is a local MAP routine. The offsets are plug-in means computed before the fit, the average over initialisations is not a posterior mean, column standardisation fixes each benchmark's noise scale, and intervals are conformal and not model-based. [inferred]
- Under this model a model row is described by an offset plus two factor scores, that is three numbers, and the fitted matrix has rank up to 4 in transformed space (1 a^T + b 1^T + U V^T). The abstract's "two numbers" refers to the Soft-Impute rank, a different parameterisation. [inferred from body:13, body:544-552, app:604-610]

### How rank 2 is established

- Evidence 1: Soft-Impute (hard rank-R SVD projection alternating with clamping observed entries) swept over rank in raw and logit space; held-out MedAPE is minimised at rank 2 in both (Figure 4, body:360-364, body:379; app:604-610). No uncertainty shown on that figure (app:498).
- Robustness: newest 17 models (latest 20% by release date) held out as a block, each predicted from older models plus two thirds of its own scores; with 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals over 17 models, logit-space minimum stays at rank 2 while "in raw space ranks 2 and 3 fall within each other's interval" and the raw curve is "flat across the first three ranks" (app:497-508).
- Evidence 2: SVD of column-mean-centred fully observed submatrices (Table 2, body:396-399):

| # Bench | # Models | Stable rank | Var top 1 | Var top 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 42 | 1.11 | 90.4% | 97.6% |
| 7 | 11 | 1.28 | 78.2% | 91.2% |
| 10 | 7 | 1.16 | 86.5% | 95.0% |
| 13 | 6 | 1.12 | 89.6% | 95.9% |

- The SVD scale (raw vs logit) and whether columns are variance-standardised are not stated; only mean-centring is described (body:408). [inferred]
- Rank is then **fixed at 2** for Soft-Impute and Bias ALS in the method comparison; the ALS grid tunes only lambda in {0.01, 0.1, 1.0} (body:516, body:555, body:588-589). Rank is tuned for NMF and PMF over {1, 2, 3, 5}: NMF selects r = 1 for every transform; PMF selects r = 3 or 5 (app:787-830). [shown]
- Factor interpretation (SVD of the rank-2 completed, column-standardised matrix): PC1 = general capability, all 133 loadings same sign (0.042 to 0.097 vs 0.087 uniform), Spearman 0.893 with mean z-scored observed score; 131 of 133 benchmarks correlate positively with PC1 score on observed entries. PC2 = domain lean (long-context and coding vs agentic search and competition math) and does not order models by strength (body:417-425, Table 3 at body:428-446).
- Finding box: "The 84 x 133 model-benchmark score matrix behaves as an effectively rank-2 prediction problem" (body:414). Limitation 4: "the rank-2 geometry is a property of the current snapshot rather than a guarantee" (body:1167).

### Evaluation protocol

- Main holdout: "For each model, we randomly hide half of its known benchmark scores, train on the remaining half (plus all other models' data)"; "3 folds per seed and 10 seeds (about 20,000 test predictions per pair)" (body:569-570). Elsewhere called "10-seed, three-fold per-model holdout" (app:157) and the block-holdout variant keeps "two thirds of its own observed scores" (app:498). The hide fraction (one half vs one third) is ambiguous; see open questions.
- Metrics: MedAPE = median of |s-hat - s| / |s| x 100% within a fold, then median over folds; MedAE = same with |s-hat - s| in raw score points; coverage = fraction of held-out entries with a finite prediction. Medians are used because errors are heavy-tailed (body:574-580).
- Hyperparameters: nested validation inside each outer fold; configuration with lowest validation MedAPE is evaluated on outer test cells (body:582-595, app:689-690).
- Candidate methods (12): benchmark mean, model mean, Bench-KNN, Model-KNN, BenchReg, ModelReg, Soft-Impute, NMF, PMF, nuclear norm, Bias ALS, 2-layer MLP (body:485-559; formulas app:539-678). 7 x 12 = 84 transform-method pairs; 203 full-coverage configurations ranked on validation (app:690).
- Model-mean baseline is x-bar_m. in standardised transformed space (app:545-549), so after un-standardising it is a benchmark offset plus a model offset: an additive two-way baseline in all but name. Main text describes it differently ("adjust the benchmark mean by each model's overall strength percentile", body:491-492). [inferred]
- LLM baseline: GPT-5.5 with five nearest-peer examples, named and blind conditions, same held-out cells (body:692-703).
- Probe-set selection: greedy forward selection of 10 benchmarks minimising pooled error over all observed cells; for a target model only probe columns stay visible in its row; **observed probe cells count as zero error** and stay in the denominator; probes are selected and scored on the same matrix. The authors state the curves "should not be read as held-out transfer estimates for a fixed universal probe set" (body:739-748). Random baseline: 10 global random orderings (body:753).
- Ranking: completed leaderboards (true scores on seen cells, predictions on held-out cells); same-benchmark pairs with at least one held-out cell and true gap at least the margin; median across benchmarks of pairwise accuracy (body:850-860).
- Temporal: 27 targets from DeepSeek-R1 through GPT-5.1 with more than 20 observed scores; train only on models released before the target; reveal k in {1, 5, 10} random scores; 10 seeds; **revealed cells contribute zero error to the pooled metric**; cells without a finite prediction are dropped (body:912-919). Window "chosen using only release metadata and matrix coverage before inspecting prediction errors" [claim, body:912].
- Reliability hypotheses: 7 benchmark-side and 9 model-side no-effect hypotheses, Spearman or paired Wilcoxon, each under MedAPE and MedAE, threshold p < 0.01, Benjamini-Hochberg at alpha = 0.01 over 32 tests rejects 17 (body:948-963, body:1088, app:1264-1269).
- Reliability estimators: ridge or small MLP trained fold-out to predict log(1 + |error|) from ensemble-spread features (8), matrix-support features, or both (hybrid) (body:1109-1132, app:1359-1386).
- Intervals: leave-fold-out split-conformal scaling; 90th percentile of |s-hat - s| / r on other folds times the risk r, centred on the point prediction (app:1390).

## Terminology and Conventions

- **BenchPress**: the logit, column-standardise, Bias ALS (R = 2, lambda = 0.1) recipe (body:651-659).
- **Score matrix**: models x benchmarks, s_mb observed, Omega the observed set (body:178, app:527).
- **Probe (set)**: benchmarks run on a new model and revealed to the predictor (body:740).
- **Seed scores**: revealed scores for a temporally held-out model (body:918).
- **MedAE / MedAPE**: median absolute (percentage) error; "within X points" in the abstract and finding boxes always means a median (body:574-579).
- **Coverage** has two meanings: fraction of cells a method can predict (body:580) and, in the reliability section, the fraction of cells kept or the interval coverage (body:1139, app:1422).
- **Stable rank**: ||M||_F^2 / sigma_1^2 (body:407).
- **Hide-half**: the per-model holdout protocol (body:971, body:1025).
- **Canonical setting**: one configuration per model and one column per benchmark family (body:192, body:266).
- "Rank" in the hypothesis-test appendix means order statistic, not matrix rank (app:1243).

## Entities and Relationships

- Code `github.com/microsoft/benchpress`; dataset `huggingface.co/datasets/microsoft/benchpress-score-matrix` (CDLA-Permissive-2.0) (main.tex title block; card).
- Compared against: Soft-Impute (Mazumder 2010), nuclear norm (Candes 2009), PMF, NMF, observational scaling laws (Ruan 2024), GPT-5.5 as predictor.
- Related low-rank findings cited: Burnell 2023 (three factors), Ilic 2024 (g-factor, 85%), Burnham 2025 (Epoch Capabilities Index: general axis plus provider residual via PCA), Ruan 2024 (body:141-145).
- Uses HELM and Every Eval Ever as external matrices (app:906-929). Cited by Helm et al. (arXiv 2611.03614) as the LRMC baseline.

## Workflows and Decision Rules

- Point prediction recipe: logit, standardise, Bias ALS rank 2 lambda 0.1, invert (body:651-659).
- Triage rule implied by the reliability section: sort predictions by hybrid risk, trust the lowest-risk fraction, run the benchmark for the rest (body:1134-1144).
- Trust conditions [claim, Finding at body:1090]: target benchmark with many observations and a correlated neighbour; target model with many observed scores and a correlated peer; training matrix containing recent models.
- Probe sets are snapshot-specific and "should be rerun" on a different matrix (body:835-837).

## Constraints and Non-Goals

- Stated limitations (body:1162-1169): no close neighbour means unreliable prediction; aggregate-only, item-level extension is future work; untested modalities; rank 2 is snapshot-specific; reliability factors are correlational and non-exhaustive; reliability layer is "a practical add-on" and better uncertainty quantification "remains open".
- Stated caveats (body:118-125): public-score heterogeneity, snapshot dependence, inferability is not redundancy of the benchmark.
- OSL comparison caveat: where a stronger model must be predicted from weaker models alone, OSL "is the better-matched tool" and that regime is outside BenchPress's target (app:899).
- Unstated assumptions detected [inferred]:
  - Held-out cells are random draws from observed cells, so reported error is for the observed-cell distribution; the 76.7% never-observed cells could differ systematically (selection on expected performance, vendor-favourable reporting).
  - Squared-error loss in standardised logit space treats cells as homoscedastic, though benchmarks have 6 to 258,090 items.
  - The source-priority rule and "setting the vendor foregrounds" favour the highest-effort vendor configuration, so the matrix is closer to best-reported than typical performance.
  - Rank evidence from fully observed submatrices uses the most popular benchmarks and models (4 x 42 down to 13 x 6), where at most 3 to 5 non-trivial components exist.
  - Cross-benchmark correlations rest on as few as 5 shared models (app:992, app:1034-1036).
  - Hyperparameter choice (lambda = 0.1) and the "near top of the leaderboard" selection look at the held-out table (body:603), though the nested validation ranking agrees (app:706).
  - Conformal validity assumes exchangeability of cells across folds; cells from the same model or benchmark are dependent.
